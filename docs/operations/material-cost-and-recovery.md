# Consume substitutes, cost the output and recover material

## Release material to a work order

Identify the work order and operation, original bill of materials item, physical item actually transferred, source and work in progress warehouses, submitted transfer row, stock unit of measure and factor, quantity and traceable lot. An ordinary transfer into work in progress changes location and warehouse accounting where the accounts differ. It leaves the company holding the material. For tracked semi-finished manufacture, release against the specific job card. For its subcontracted operation, issue to the supplier through the subcontracting order.

A new substitution on an outward transfer requires the work order to allow alternatives and the pair (original, substitute) to be active in the permitted direction. A two-way pair is reversible. The master rejects self-substitution, duplicates and stock/non-stock mismatches. Both original and, for a two-way pair, alternate must allow alternatives. Other differences such as serial/batch handling issue a warning at master validation and remain subject to transaction-specific tracking controls. Downstream consumption or return honours a pair already used on a submitted non-return transfer for this work order. A later edit to the master must not strand work in progress physically released under an earlier valid decision.

Split demand between original and alternatives in proportion to their positive available quantities. When all are depleted, the calculation falls back to the positive quantities originally transferred. If those also sum to zero, propose no split. Round all but the last positive candidate to quantity precision, cap them by the remaining request, and give the last candidate the residual. With physical remnants 4 of A and 2 of B, a demand of 3 produces 2 A and 1 B. Choosing an alternative changes the physical stock ledger, identity and valuation. It does not create a requisition or rewrite the bill of materials.

## Determine the basis for output cost

| Situation | Source of raw-material value | Posting constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Standard manufacture with outgoing raw materials | Sum quantity × effective outward basic rate for source rows, with the configured detail precision | Issue the actual items, including substitutes, at their own ledger costs. |
| Separate consumption enabled, work order has submitted consumption vouchers, and raw-material costing from those vouchers is enabled | Sum valuation rate × stock quantity on submitted Material Consumption for Manufacture entries for that work order | Manufacture must contain only finished/secondary items. Another effective manufacture for the same order is disallowed in this branch. |
| Material-consumption mode, costing from separate entries disabled, zero outgoing cost | A bill of materials estimate is available only when there is no known consumption basis. Actual zero-valued input stays zero. | Replacing free material with the latest bill of materials estimate would create phantom asset value. |
| Own-priced and percentage outputs | Price own-cost outputs first, then use bill of materials allocations against the residual pool. Preserve secondary identity by recipe row/type where item codes recur. | Manually valued finished quantities and their cost must be removed before determining the residual automatic finished rate. |

For material cost 240, one manual by-product valued at 20 and percentage allocations of 75% to the primary output and 25% to a co-product, the discrete pool is 220. Ten primary units receive value 165 at 16.50 and two co-product units receive 55 at 27.50. Together with the manual by-product 20 they exhaust 240. This example assumes one company currency, agreed allocation percentages, zero additional costs and no manual primary rate. If ten planned primary units incur two process-loss units and only eight accepted units carry that same 165, the primary rate is 20.625 before configured rounding. Loss increases unit cost without creating two separately stocked finished goods.

In tracked semi-finished production, convert each operation's reported loss to work-order finished units as operation loss × work-order quantity / effective operation quantity, applying loss precision per row. An operation with zero denominator contributes zero. Sum converted loss across operations. In ordinary production the work order instead sums process loss on submitted manufacture stock entries. If a plan already used a recorded loss to justify a replacement order, lowering it requires revalidation under the plan-row lock. Rebuilding costing must not double-book the original loss.

## A movement and accounting trace

Assume separate inventory accounts for Stores, work in progress and Finished, perpetual inventory, prior receipts at the indicated rates, no taxes, no other overhead, no process loss and no by-products. The ordinary manufacture entry consumes its inputs itself. Quantities are physical stock units. The ledger table gives the net change per account from each business event before account merging.

| Submitted event | Stock ledger effect | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Release to work in progress | Stores −6 A at 10 and −4 substitute B at 15. Work in progress +6 A and +4 B | work in progress 120 | Stores 120 |
| Manufacture 4 finished units | work in progress −5 A at 10 and −3 B at 15; Finished +4 at 23.75 | Finished 95 | work in progress 95 |
| Close or complete work order. Return unused inputs | work in progress −1 A and −1 B; Stores +1 A and +1 B | Stores 25 | work in progress 25 |

work in progress began and ended at zero for the run. Stores is down 95 and Finished is up 95. The return validates the work order is Completed or Closed. Its cap per actual item is transferred stock minus consumption in manufacture/consumption entries minus prior effective store returns. Proposed returned row stock quantities use transaction quantity × conversion factor. A request to send back 2 B would exceed the single B left in work in progress and must fail before posting. Reversing the return, when permitted, moves the same 25 into work in progress and restores its returnable amount.

Disassembly is a separate physical event that issues finished units and recovers components. The manufactured goods can already have a stock history. Read and validate the specific manufacture reference, check the same work order when supplied, and cap the request by source finished quantity minus submitted prior disassembly. The actual finished quantity consumed in stock units must match the requested disassembly quantity. Compare each component to its source row, validate all required rows are present, reject unrelated items, and preserve stock-unit conversions and lot identities. The source-less bill of materials route has its own validation path. A return of unconsumed material cannot be used to recover stock already embodied in finished goods.

For a source making 3 finished units with 5 indivisible component pieces, three single-unit disassemblies recover 1, 2 and 2 pieces. The calculation floors the cumulative whole-unit entitlement before subtracting previously recovered pieces. A fourth disassembly is rejected. Reversing or altering history requires checking downstream dependency, batch and subsequent-value implications. The recovered value is a stock-valuation decision. Quantity conservation alone does not establish that a disassembly reverses the original general ledger legs at their historical rates.

## Corrective jobs and equipment repairs

Corrective job cards record the production work order and corrective work separately from normal throughput. Submitted corrective minutes × the corrective card's own hourly rate feed corrective operating cost. The regular operating-card aggregate excludes them. The stock-transfer and operation rollups have dedicated tests for corrective cards. Cancelling a corrective card recomputes the work-order aggregate. Repairing equipment that the company owns uses an Asset Repair with invoice allocation rows and spare-part issues instead.

A repair needs an eligible asset and a completion date when completed. Submission requires Completed status. Sold, scrapped and capitalized source assets cannot be repaired here. A fully depreciated asset clears the capitalize-cost and life-increase selections in this path. Validate each supplier invoice's submitted status, expense account and amount available for allocation, and reject duplicate invoice/account combinations. Record downtime from failure and completion. To consume spares, create a linked Material Issue at completion time carrying serial/batch and accounting dimensions. Refresh repair rows from the posted issue's valuation and amount, replacing preliminary estimates. Total repair cost equals allocated invoice expenses plus effective spare cost.

For a 75 supplier invoice expense allocation and two spares issued at ledger cost 15 each, actual repair cost is 105 even if the draft estimated each spare at 12. With capitalization enabled, expense credits of 75 and 30 transfer into the fixed asset debit of 105. The issue separately debits repair stock expense 30 and credits warehouse stock 30 in a perpetual company. Net stock decreases by 30, the asset increases by 105, and the pre-existing supplier expense decreases by 75. The existing payable remains attached to the invoice. Without capitalization, the stock issue still records physical consumption and expense. Where applicable adjust each finance book's depreciable value and life, then reschedule depreciation. On cancellation cancel the linked issue, reverse posted capitalization, adjust life and reschedule as applicable, cancel the repair's identity bundles, and record the asset activity. For older non-depreciating assets the cancellation path checks whether the original repair had actually raised value after depreciation before reducing that field.
