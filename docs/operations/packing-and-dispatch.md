# Packing, trip assignment and dispatch

## Packing a draft delivery

The operator maps a draft delivery note to a packing slip. For an ordinary delivery row the mapper uses its transaction unit, preserves its row ID and proposes ordered delivery quantity less packed quantity. For an enabled product bundle the physical packed component rows become the source. The commercial parent is excluded from the ordinary row mapping. Each slip row needs a positive quantity and a draft reference with the same parent delivery and item. Accumulate all slip rows per referenced row and check their sum against the remaining quantity. Two packages can each fit separately while their combined quantities exceed the available line.

The start package number must be positive. An omitted end defaults to the start. An explicit end must be at least the start. Validate explicit ranges for inclusive overlaps with submitted slips on the same delivery. The suggested next number is the highest submitted end plus one. For weights, fill an absent row weight from the item weight per unit multiplied by the delivery row conversion factor, or factor one for a packed component. All rows must share a weight unit for summation. Net package weight is the sum of row quantity times row weight, rounded to two decimals. A missing or zero gross weight defaults to the net. Package numbering does not multiply item quantity or weight.

Submitting a slip updates packed progress on the corresponding delivery or component rows. Cancelling it refreshes the same progress. Once the delivery note is submitted an operator cannot cancel the slip independently. Cancellation initiated by its delivery uses a separate path to cancel dependent slips. Retain packed component row IDs across edits to unrelated delivery rows so submitted slips remain traceable. For example, two boxes of twelve, each stock unit weighing one kilogram, gave a net weight of 24 kg. Packs exceeding a common source row's remaining quantity were rejected. Editing a draft delivery retained its packed component IDs. Cancelling a submitted delivery also cancelled its submitted slip.

## Assigning a vehicle and driver

On save, the trip locks referenced delivery-note rows and checks company and ownership. A note already assigned to a different draft or submitted trip blocks the new assignment. Where both a customer and contact are selected, their relationship must exist. On submission a driver is required and must be active. A licence with an expiry date must cover the departure date, including equality. Referenced draft notes block submission. Once submitted, zero visited stops in a nonempty list gives Scheduled, some visited gives In Transit, and all visited gives Completed. Cancellation sets Cancelled and releases the trip's claim.

The trip may fill driver, driver name, vehicle, transport reference and transport date on a linked delivery. On a subsequent edit, update empty fields or fields that still match the trip's current or previous values. On stop removal, deletion or cancellation, release a note only if it still identifies this trip. Clear a nonempty transport field only if it matches a trip value. A separately supplied vehicle number or carrier document reference survives linking and unlinking. Equality is used to infer ownership for each field. The business record does not store a separate provenance token per field. Two independently entered identical values therefore cannot be distinguished by this comparison alone.

## Route planning and notification

Locked stops act as boundaries when optimising a route. Use each returned leg's distance in metres times the configured conversion factor for the stop distance. Estimated arrival is departure plus leg duration in seconds. The next departure is the arrival plus the configured stop delay in minutes. Include a final return-home leg in the trip distance when the route supplies one, and omit its arrival from the customer stops. In a worked route, a departure at 08:00, three 1,000 metre legs of ten minutes each, two customer stops and a five-minute dwell produce customer arrivals of 08:10 and 08:25, and a total distance of 3 km including the return. When stops are locked, retain their position and accumulate each route segment only once.

Dispatch notification requires a submitted trip, access to the trip and read access to **every** linked delivery. Check these before sending the first message. A configured email template provides the subject and body. Each stop with a contact email receives its own message with that stop's submitted delivery rows and optional printed delivery note. The stop records its recipient. If any messages were queued, the trip sets the notification flag. The flag records that at least one message was queued. Delivery confirmation belongs to the recipient message. Identify re-sends by trip, stop, recipient and dispatch action. Record the recipient’s language on the issued message.
