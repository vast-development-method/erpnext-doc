# Invoice tax, trade discount and rounding

Calculate from the ordered invoice items, ordered charge rows, mapped item-tax exceptions, discount kind and basis, transaction and company currencies, field precisions and rounding mode. A reimplementation must preserve the originating item-row identity, even when several rows refer to one product. The observable result includes each item's net amount, each charge's after-discount amount, document total, receivable, general ledger posting and item-wise tax details. The [commercial tax chapter](taxation-and-pricing.md) describes the full tax model. The cases here fix commercial rounding to two places and disable whole-invoice rounding for the rounding profiles.

## Included prices and eligibility

For each item compute each included row as a fraction of the unknown net price plus a per-unit intercept. A charge on prior amount inherits only that charge. A charge on prior running total inherits the item's net and all amounts through that earlier row. Sum the included fractions and subtract the summed intercept times quantity from the printed amount before dividing by one plus the sum of fractions. Keep the unrounded taxable base. An actual fixed document charge cannot be included in a printed item rate. An included previous-amount charge requires its referenced row to be included. An included previous-total charge requires every row through the referenced row to be included. Refuse self or forward references, a previous-row reference in the first row, and a valuation-only included charge.

For 100 units at 50.00 printed each, including 14% of net plus 5.00 per unit, net is `(5000 − 500) / 1.14 = 3947.37`. Percentage tax is 552.63 and the unit charge is 500.00. A net-basis discount of 100.00 reduces net to 3847.37, percentage tax to 538.63 and amount due to 4886.00. A grand-total-basis discount of 100.00 uses a discountable total of 4500.00, since the 500.00 unit charge stays fixed. It reduces the net to 3859.65 and percentage tax to 540.35, yielding 4900.00. These cases use the amount after discount in tax general ledger rows.

## Which item gets the extra cent?

In document-level tax mode group by the complete sorted item-tax map. For an inclusive group accumulate the unrounded net of each applicable item in row order. Each new item's net is the rounded cumulative unrounded amount less the group's previously allocated rounded amount. Quotation alternatives do not take part in this carry. For two identical rows of 379.00 inclusive of 21%, the first net is 313.22 and the second 313.23. Net 626.45 plus tax 131.55 equals 758.00. In row-wise tax mode both rows have net 313.22 and tax 65.78 each, yielding total net 626.44 and tax 131.56. No cash-rounding adjustment is present. The cent belongs in the relevant item and tax amounts.

Document-level mode with a grand-total trade discount retains unrounded item net for the allocation whenever included taxes are present. At each item allocate its share of the discountable basis, round the new amount, and reconcile the running group total. When a correction increases the item's new net by one cent, decrease the item's allocated discount by one cent as well. Two 379.00 lines with a 10% grand-total discount produce net 563.80, tax 118.40 and amount due 682.20. With twenty identical rows the expected amounts become 5638.02, 1183.98 and 6822.00. These values catch accumulated per-line rounding that a single-line fixture cannot reveal.

## Two dependent taxes on differently exempt lines

Take two sales lines of 100.00. The first is eligible for a fixed 10.00 tax but exempt from a dependent 10% levy. The second is exempt from that fixed tax but eligible for the levy. Allocate the 10.00 to the first line. If the levy is on **previous-row amount**, its only eligible line has previous tax of zero. Post receivable debit 210.00, sales credit 200.00 and fixed-tax credit 10.00. There is no levy row in the general ledger. If it is on **previous-row total**, the second line has taxable running total 100.00, levy 10.00, and receivable 220.00. Calculating the levy on the document-wide first tax or splitting that first tax onto exempt lines produces a different receivable.

Positive fixed tax on eligible lines with zero aggregate net still needs to be allocated. An example with two zero-priced eligible lines puts 0.00 on the first and the full 10.00 on the last. When the last item is exempt, the last **eligible** item takes the 10.00. The tax detail records the eligible item and tax-row identities. A full return of the current previous-total case reverses 220.00. A return of a historic record stored as 220.50 by an older split recalculates the return under the current policy at −220.00. The 0.50 historical difference can remain; treat identical original and return totals as an assertion only for original rows that used the same allocation.
