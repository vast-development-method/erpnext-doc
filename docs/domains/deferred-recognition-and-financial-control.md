# Deferred recognition and financial control

The linked definitions contain the complete field, relationship, choice, default and permission catalogs. This chapter supplies their financial meaning, calculations, lifecycle decisions and accounting consequences. No external source files are needed to follow these contracts.

## Recognition and claim are independent

An invoice establishes a claim or obligation at its document date. Revenue or expense recognition can be spread over the period in which the service is provided. Each deferred invoice line therefore retains its own service start date, service end date, optional stop date, deferred account, eventual income or expense account, net amount in transaction and company currency, cost center, project and accounting dimensions. Payment due dates are independent of service dates. Do not derive recognition from when money is collected or paid.

A deferred sales line initially credits deferred income. A deferred purchase line initially debits deferred expense. Recognition of income debits deferred income and credits income; recognition of expense debits expense and credits deferred expense. Recognition does not create another customer or supplier claim. It retains invoice and invoice-line identity and links the recognition run that caused the entry.

## Interval progression and recovery

The next recognition interval starts on the service start date, or one day after the latest applicable recorded recognition date. Discovery considers direct ledger recognition and relevant linked journals, including a draft journal that has reserved the interval. The interval normally ends at month end, capped by service end, stop date where applicable, and processing cutoff. An interval with start later than end produces no booking. When several intervals are due, calculation continues until the cutoff or final interval, but stops on an accounting error.

Recognition can create direct ledger entries or linked journals according to policy. Journal creation and journal submission are separate choices: a generated draft is not recognized financial income. Persist the distinction between calculated, journal-created, posted and failed outcomes. Repeating a run must inspect existing interval evidence rather than begin again at service start and double-book the service. The inspected direct-posting path commits successful work and reports failures; therefore the overall multi-line run is not proven to be one indivisible accounting transaction. Recovery should identify the exact completed line intervals.

If an interval ends within a frozen accounting period, the reviewed processing moves its ledger posting date to the final day of the month containing the day after the frozen-through date. It keeps internal progress against the original service interval. Several service intervals can therefore share one permitted posting date. They remain distinct recognition effects, and must not be mistaken for a duplicate event based only on posting date.

The exact end-date decision order is significant. First choose the last day of the month containing the next interval start. If that date reaches or passes service end, replace it with service end and mark the interval final. Only when it has not reached service end is a service stop checked and allowed to mark the interval final. Finally cap the selected end by the processing cutoff. The final marker is not cleared by that last cap. As a result, a cutoff inside the final service month can cause the residual to be recognized before the service end. Compatibility example: a 900.00 service runs January 1 through March 31 in a nonleap year; after January and February have recognized 590.00, processing through March 15 recognizes the remaining 310.00 at March 15 under this decision order. A replacement choosing to accrue only 150.00 through March 15 is adopting a corrected recognition policy, not reproducing this calculation. Record the selected policy and use one consistently.

Recognition progress and recognized financial balance also have different selection rules. Progress discovery selects the latest matching deferred-account posting for company, invoice kind, invoice identity and invoice-line identity; it does not restrict that search to the recognition debit or credit side. It also considers noncancelled linked journals, including drafts. Already booked value, by contrast, sums the deferred-income debit side for sales or deferred-expense credit side for purchases, plus corresponding amounts in linked noncancelled journals. Draft journal amounts therefore reserve recognition capacity while still contributing nothing to the general ledger. This distinction must be retained by any compatible progress query, and a draft must remain visible as pending recognition rather than appearing as posted income or expense.

The run record contains company, recognition kind, optional account, start date, end date and posting date; reject an end date before its start date. The [recognition run definition](../../schemas/data/record-types/process_deferred_accounting.json) and [journal definition](../../schemas/data/record-types/journal_entry.json) provide the persistent fields. Direct recognition rows retain the run as their settled-against reference and the invoice as their owning voucher. Cancelling the run reverses those linked direct recognition entries. Generated journals use a separate run link; their cancellation must be handled through their journal lifecycle and must not be presumed complete merely because direct run entries were reversed.

## Daily recognition arithmetic

Total service days equals service-end date less service-start date plus one. Booking days equals booking-end date less booking-start date plus one. For a nonfinal interval, company recognition equals company net amount multiplied by booking days divided by service days, rounded to company-net precision. Where the recognition account currency equals company currency, account amount equals that company amount. Otherwise calculate transaction net amount multiplied by the same day ratio and round at transaction-net precision.

For the final interval, replace the fraction calculation with total line amount less already recognized amount, separately in company and account currency. This absorbs accumulated rounding error. A final interval caused by service stop follows this residual calculation in the reviewed function; do not presume that stopping service simply abandons the unrecognized balance. Any desired refund, write-off or revised service amount needs its own business event.

Derived fixture: service January 1 through March 31 in a nonleap year has 90 days. Net value 900.00 produces January recognition 310.00, February 280.00 and final March 310.00. With net 1000.00, January is 344.44 and February 311.11; the final interval is residual 344.45. The daily method is not equivalent to three equal monthly charges.

## Monthly recognition arithmetic

The monthly method counts calendar months touched by the interval, then multiplies that count by a prorate factor. The factor is date difference between service end and service start divided by date difference from the first day of the starting month to the last day of the ending month. Unlike the daily method, these differences omit the inclusive extra day. Round the resulting effective month count to one decimal place.

A nonfinal month's base charge is company net amount divided by effective month count, rounded to the company amount precision and capped so cumulative recognized value cannot exceed line value. If the account is foreign currency, perform the analogous calculation in transaction amount. A partial booking month then multiplies the charge by its rounded-to-one-decimal partial-month ratio. The partial ratio uses booking-end less booking-start divided by ending-month last day less starting-month first day. The final interval again uses exact residual.

This monthly algorithm is deliberately recorded as observed. Replacing it with an assumed thirty-day month, exact calendar-month fractions, or inclusive daily proportions changes results. One-day services and partial intervals at month boundaries require explicit denominator and rounding fixtures before a replacement exposes them through this method.

For clarity, let \(M\) be the number of calendar months touched by the service, \(D\) the date difference between service end and start, and \(C\) the date difference between the final month's last day and the first month's first day. Effective months are \(\operatorname{round}(M D/C,1)\). The nonfinal full-month company charge is \(\operatorname{round}(\operatorname{CompanyNet}/\operatorname{EffectiveMonths},p)\), capped by remaining company value. For a partial month, multiply that rounded charge by the booking date-difference ratio rounded to one decimal place. The partial multiplication is not itself explicitly rounded again by this calculation; the destination monetary fields supply their normal storage precision. Final recognition uses the remaining value instead of dividing by effective months.

An effective month count of zero is not a valid divisor. A one-day service has date difference zero and must use the final-residual branch when immediately completed; any nonfinal request reaching a zero divisor must fail before posting. This is a mathematical input boundary, not permission to substitute an arbitrary one-month denominator. The daily method has an inclusive one-day denominator of one and does not share this zero-divisor issue.

Numerical acceptance example: service value 3000.00 for May 1 through July 31, monthly recognition, invoice dated July 1, and books frozen through May 31. Processing recognition through June 30 creates two 1000.00 income credits and two 1000.00 deferred-income debits on June 30, representing the May and June service periods. The invoice's original receivable debit 3000.00 and deferred-income credit 3000.00 remain. Cancelling that recognition run removes its effective recognition consequences while retaining the invoice posting.

## Budget controls

Current budget validation groups by accounting dimension kind, value and financial account. It includes default cost center and project plus configured dimensions. A budget on a tree group can be expanded to descendant dimension values. For each matched key, retain annual budget, accumulated-period budget, outstanding request amount, outstanding order amount, current actual journal amount and booked actual expense. These are independently inspectable quantities, not one opaque available-budget value.

Outstanding purchase-order amount is the sum of line amount less billed amount for qualifying submitted, nonclosed orders. Outstanding purchase-request amount is unconverted-to-order stock quantity multiplied by request rate for qualifying submitted purchase requests. Actual expense is effective company ledger debits less credits for the account, dimension, company and financial year. The current proposed journal amount is added before persistence. Request and order checks occur after their document submission path has already included those documents in the query, so their current amounts are not added a second time.

Individual annual excess equals existing relevant amount plus current relevant amount less annual budget. Individual accumulated-period excess uses the same amount against accumulated budget. Cumulative excess includes outstanding requests, unbilled orders, actual expense and current changes together. Positive excess triggers the selected warning or stop. A configured exception-approver role downgrades the action to warning.

The [budget distribution](../../schemas/data/record-types/budget_distribution.json) controls the accumulated amount. Its supported frequencies advance by one, three, six or twelve months. Each generated period starts on the first day of its starting month and ends on the last day of the final month in that frequency, capped by budget end. Equal distribution assigns each period \(100/N\) percent of the budget, where \(N\) is the number of periods. Store both its amount and percentage at three decimal places. A manually weighted distribution whose only change is total budget recalculates each amount as its retained percentage times the new budget divided by 100, rounded to three places. Manually retained rows reject an amount-total discrepancy or percentage-total discrepancy whose absolute value, rounded to two places, exceeds 0.10. This is separate from journal balancing.

Accumulated budget is the sum of distribution amounts whose start dates are on or before the transaction date. It is not prorated day by day and does not wait for a distribution period to finish. For a 12000.00 annual budget split into four equal quarters, a transaction on January 1 can use 3000.00 under accumulated-period enforcement; a transaction on April 1 can use 6000.00. At exactly the applicable budget there is no excess. Any positive excess follows the configured stop, warn or ignore action.

Derived cumulative fixture: annual budget 10000.00, outstanding purchase requests 1500.00, unbilled orders 3000.00, actual expense 5000.00 and proposed actual expense 600.00 produce cumulative exposure 10100.00 and excess 100.00. Under stop policy an ordinary user cannot complete the posting; under warning policy it can complete while reporting the excess. Whether the invoice simultaneously reduces the order exposure depends on the business-event ordering and must be included in an integrated purchase-to-pay fixture.

The inspected current controller explicitly notes unresolved treatment of a proposed entry set spanning multiple financial years and potential duplicate budget keys. A replacement must define and test those cases before claiming equivalent complete budget enforcement. A recommended additional invariant is that budget checks and commitment updates for one purchase event are atomic; it is proposed here because static inspection alone does not prove the concurrency guarantee.

Those two ambiguous cases have concrete observed mechanics. A proposed ledger set takes company and financial-year context from its first row. Matching budgets are stored by dimension kind, dimension value and account; a later record with the same key replaces an earlier one, without an explicit selection ordering. There is therefore no deterministic cross-year or duplicate-budget precedence contract to reproduce. A safe replacement policy is to partition a request by company and financial year before budget validation and reject overlapping effective budgets for one key. These are explicitly proposed validation rules; they must not be described as behaviour already guaranteed by the reviewed system.

## Period closing and control totals

Financial close verifies the selected interval, company and closing account, disallows closing before a later close, and guards frozen books. Current processing computes grouped account balances across worker partitions but posts final company closing effects under the original closing record. The final output has two components: reversing profit-and-loss entries and closing balances for later reporting. A report must distinguish in-progress close, completed close, failed close and cancelled close.

The stock-related financial-close gate applies only when perpetual inventory is enabled and a noncancelled stock movement exists on or before the closing date. In that case stock asset account balance and stock valuation as of close date must match after both are rounded to ledger debit precision. The required stock closing covers the entire company and exact closing date, must be completed, and must be regenerated if stock history through that date has changed after its preparation. This prevents approving an accounting close using an obsolete inventory valuation snapshot.

Acceptance includes daily and monthly recognition fixtures, leap-year daily denominators, one-day service validation, stopped service, frozen-period shifted dates, repeated recognition run, draft recognition journal, cancelled recognition run, partially failed batch, budget exact equality, warning versus stop, exceptional authority, two competing budget-consuming operations and closing against stale stock history. These tests need posted balances and interval evidence, not merely document status assertions.

## Recognition reconciliation and numerical acceptance

The acceptance case `daily_deferred_income_with_final_residual` fixes nonleap January through March, company and account currency equal, value 1000.00 and two decimal places. Invoice recognition creates receivable debit 1000.00 and deferred-income credit 1000.00. January transfers 344.44 and February 311.11 from deferred income to income. March transfers the exact remaining 344.45. After the last event deferred income is zero, total recognized income is 1000.00 and the unpaid customer claim remains 1000.00. Collection, if any, is a separate event.

The corresponding expense direction starts with deferred-expense debit and supplier-payable credit; each interval debits expense and credits deferred expense. Both directions require interval identity, not only a monthly posting date, because frozen periods can move different intervals to one date. Reconcile total line amount against posted recognition, draft-journal reserved recognition and unreserved future recognition separately; a draft does not enter recognized income or expense even when it reserves future processing.

Recognition run cancellation must identify direct postings reversed and generated journals still requiring their own lifecycle action. After a partially completed run, retry only intervals lacking a durable completed or draft-reserved result. The present specification's cutoff-final-marker behavior remains an observed compatibility rule; the acceptance case `deferred_final_month_cutoff_boundary` records its early residual separately from a proposed daily-through-cutoff alternative.
