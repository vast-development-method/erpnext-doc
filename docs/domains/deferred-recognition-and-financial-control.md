# Deferred recognition and financial control

## Recognition and claim are independent

An invoice establishes a claim or obligation at its document date. Revenue or expense recognition can be spread over the period in which the service is provided. Each deferred invoice line therefore retains its own service start date, service end date, optional stop date, deferred account, eventual income or expense account, net amount in transaction and company currency, cost center, project and accounting dimensions. Payment due dates are independent of service dates. Do not derive recognition from when money is collected or paid. Evidence: source-artifact-d72b4d93df58836c77ea lines 373–415.

A deferred sales line initially credits deferred income. A deferred purchase line initially debits deferred expense. Recognition of income debits deferred income and credits income; recognition of expense debits expense and credits deferred expense. Recognition does not create another customer or supplier claim. It retains invoice and invoice-line identity and links the recognition run that caused the entry. Evidence: source-artifact-2bea4912baee8be7d3a4 lines 377–406; source-artifact-f873241b94f87c01af73 lines 337–357; source-artifact-d72b4d93df58836c77ea lines 519–578.

## Interval progression and recovery

The next recognition interval starts on the service start date, or one day after the latest applicable recorded recognition date. Discovery considers direct ledger recognition and relevant linked journals, including a draft journal that has reserved the interval. The interval normally ends at month end, capped by service end, stop date where applicable, and processing cutoff. An interval with start later than end produces no booking. When several intervals are due, calculation continues until the cutoff or final interval, but stops on an accounting error. Evidence: source-artifact-d72b4d93df58836c77ea lines 149–222; source-artifact-d72b4d93df58836c77ea lines 373–484.

Recognition can create direct ledger entries or linked journals according to policy. Journal creation and journal submission are separate choices: a generated draft is not recognized financial income. Persist the distinction between calculated, journal-created, posted and failed outcomes. Repeating a run must inspect existing interval evidence rather than begin again at service start and double-book the service. The inspected direct-posting path commits successful work and reports failures; therefore the overall multi-line run is not proven to be one indivisible accounting transaction. Recovery should identify the exact completed line intervals.

If an interval ends within a frozen accounting period, the reviewed processing moves its ledger posting date to the final day of the month containing the day after the frozen-through date. It keeps internal progress against the original service interval. Several service intervals can therefore share one permitted posting date. They remain distinct recognition effects, and must not be mistaken for a duplicate event based only on posting date. Evidence: source-artifact-d72b4d93df58836c77ea lines 418–457.

## Daily recognition arithmetic

Total service days equals service-end date less service-start date plus one. Booking days equals booking-end date less booking-start date plus one. For a nonfinal interval, company recognition equals company net amount multiplied by booking days divided by service days, rounded to company-net precision. Where the recognition account currency equals company currency, account amount equals that company amount. Otherwise calculate transaction net amount multiplied by the same day ratio and round at transaction-net precision.

For the final interval, replace the fraction calculation with total line amount less already recognized amount, separately in company and account currency. This absorbs accumulated rounding error. A final interval caused by service stop follows this residual calculation in the reviewed function; do not presume that stopping service simply abandons the unrecognized balance. Any desired refund, write-off or revised service amount needs its own business event. Evidence: source-artifact-d72b4d93df58836c77ea lines 280–303; source-artifact-d72b4d93df58836c77ea lines 209–216; source-artifact-d72b4d93df58836c77ea lines 399–415.

Derived fixture: service January 1 through March 31 in a nonleap year has 90 days. Net value 900.00 produces January recognition 310.00, February 280.00 and final March 310.00. With net 1000.00, January is 344.44 and February 311.11; the final interval is residual 344.45. The daily method is not equivalent to three equal monthly charges.

## Monthly recognition arithmetic

The monthly method counts calendar months touched by the interval, then multiplies that count by a prorate factor. The factor is date difference between service end and service start divided by date difference from the first day of the starting month to the last day of the ending month. Unlike the daily method, these differences omit the inclusive extra day. Round the resulting effective month count to one decimal place.

A nonfinal month's base charge is company net amount divided by effective month count, rounded to the company amount precision and capped so cumulative recognized value cannot exceed line value. If the account is foreign currency, perform the analogous calculation in transaction amount. A partial booking month then multiplies the charge by its rounded-to-one-decimal partial-month ratio. The partial ratio uses booking-end less booking-start divided by ending-month last day less starting-month first day. The final interval again uses exact residual. Evidence: source-artifact-d72b4d93df58836c77ea lines 225–277.

This monthly algorithm is deliberately recorded as observed. Replacing it with an assumed thirty-day month, exact calendar-month fractions, or inclusive daily proportions changes results. One-day services and partial intervals at month boundaries require explicit denominator and rounding fixtures before a replacement exposes them through this method.

Source test expectation: service value 3000.00 for May 1 through July 31, monthly recognition, invoice dated July 1, and books frozen through May 31. Processing recognition through June 30 creates two 1000.00 income credits and two 1000.00 deferred-income debits on June 30, representing the May and June service periods. The invoice's original receivable debit 3000.00 and deferred-income credit 3000.00 remain. Cancelling that recognition run removes its effective recognition consequences while retaining the invoice posting. Evidence: source-artifact-feccc46c92e7dd299eb3 lines 16–76.

## Budget controls

Current budget validation groups by accounting dimension kind, value and financial account. It includes default cost center and project plus configured dimensions. A budget on a tree group can be expanded to descendant dimension values. For each matched key, retain annual budget, accumulated-period budget, outstanding request amount, outstanding order amount, current actual journal amount and booked actual expense. These are independently inspectable quantities, not one opaque available-budget value. Evidence: source-artifact-735d0d4f56754c6336a5 lines 13–180.

Outstanding purchase-order amount is the sum of line amount less billed amount for qualifying submitted, nonclosed orders. Outstanding purchase-request amount is unconverted-to-order stock quantity multiplied by request rate for qualifying submitted purchase requests. Actual expense is effective company ledger debits less credits for the account, dimension, company and financial year. The current proposed journal amount is added before persistence. Request and order checks occur after their document submission path has already included those documents in the query, so their current amounts are not added a second time. Evidence: source-artifact-735d0d4f56754c6336a5 lines 82–110; source-artifact-735d0d4f56754c6336a5 lines 182–282.

Individual annual excess equals existing relevant amount plus current relevant amount less annual budget. Individual accumulated-period excess uses the same amount against accumulated budget. Cumulative excess includes outstanding requests, unbilled orders, actual expense and current changes together. Positive excess triggers the selected warning or stop. A configured exception-approver role downgrades the action to warning. Evidence: source-artifact-735d0d4f56754c6336a5 lines 295–463.

Derived cumulative fixture: annual budget 10000.00, outstanding purchase requests 1500.00, unbilled orders 3000.00, actual expense 5000.00 and proposed actual expense 600.00 produce cumulative exposure 10100.00 and excess 100.00. Under stop policy an ordinary user cannot complete the posting; under warning policy it can complete while reporting the excess. Whether the invoice simultaneously reduces the order exposure depends on the business-event ordering and must be included in an integrated purchase-to-pay fixture.

The inspected current controller explicitly notes unresolved treatment of a proposed entry set spanning multiple financial years and potential duplicate budget keys. A replacement must define and test those cases before claiming equivalent complete budget enforcement. A recommended additional invariant is that budget checks and commitment updates for one purchase event are atomic; it is proposed here because static inspection alone does not prove the concurrency guarantee.

## Period closing and control totals

Financial close verifies the selected interval, company and closing account, disallows closing before a later close, and guards frozen books. Current processing computes grouped account balances across worker partitions but posts final company closing effects under the original closing record. The final output has two components: reversing profit-and-loss entries and closing balances for later reporting. A report must distinguish in-progress close, completed close, failed close and cancelled close. Evidence: source-artifact-212c77436a097bcedef5 lines 73–164; source-artifact-212c77436a097bcedef5 lines 274–289; source-artifact-212c77436a097bcedef5 lines 837–950.

The stock-related financial-close gate applies only when perpetual inventory is enabled and a noncancelled stock movement exists on or before the closing date. In that case stock asset account balance and stock valuation as of close date must match after both are rounded to ledger debit precision. The required stock closing covers the entire company and exact closing date, must be completed, and must be regenerated if stock history through that date has changed after its preparation. This prevents approving an accounting close using an obsolete inventory valuation snapshot. Evidence: source-artifact-212c77436a097bcedef5 lines 159–272.

Acceptance includes daily and monthly recognition fixtures, leap-year daily denominators, one-day service validation, stopped service, frozen-period shifted dates, repeated recognition run, draft recognition journal, cancelled recognition run, partially failed batch, budget exact equality, warning versus stop, exceptional authority, two competing budget-consuming operations and closing against stale stock history. These tests need posted balances and interval evidence, not merely document status assertions.
