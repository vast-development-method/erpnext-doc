# Treasury and reconciliation

## Separate money movement, allocation, and clearance

Treasury distinguishes cash movement, settlement of a business claim, and bank-statement clearance. A submitted payment or journal records money in accounting. An allocation links that payment to invoices, orders, advances, or other supported claims. A bank transaction records an imported or entered statement event and reconciles it with posted vouchers. Matching a statement to an already posted payment must not create another bank debit or credit. Evidence: source-artifact-fba0a672de5ef7f8acba lines 23–38; source-artifact-a36a66a3f4375c8d5d7d lines 482–534; source-artifact-93f4d60a990dafc3fca9 lines 153–236.

The payment record preserves payment kind, company, source account, destination account, source and destination currencies, paid and received amounts, their company values, source and target conversion rates, party kind and identity, reference number and date, reference allocations, deductions, included and excluded charges, difference amount, lifecycle state, and reconciliation links. Statement records retain bank account, currency, transaction date, deposit, withdrawal, bank reference, counterparty hints, fee fields, allocation lines, remaining amount, clearance evidence and reconciliation status.

## Payment directions and monetary arithmetic

Payment kinds are receipt, payment and internal transfer. A receipt ordinarily debits destination bank and credits the party account. A payment ordinarily credits source bank and debits the party account. An internal transfer credits the source and debits the destination without requiring a trading-party claim. The same source and destination account must be rejected by the payment validation contract. A matching-currency nontransfer sets received amount equal to paid amount, and matching currencies reject received amount above paid amount. Evidence: source-artifact-151c197ffc6d8c7575df lines 173–215; source-artifact-151c197ffc6d8c7575df lines 975–989; source-artifact-fba0a672de5ef7f8acba lines 172–208.

Company paid amount equals paid amount multiplied by source conversion and rounded to its company precision. Company received amount equals received amount multiplied by target conversion and rounded independently. Reference allocated company amount uses the payment's party-side rate, while the reference's historical rate remains stored to compute the separate exchange difference. For a receipt, party-side rate is source rate; for a payment it is target rate. Order advances deliberately do not create a reference exchange gain or loss in this calculation. Evidence: source-artifact-151c197ffc6d8c7575df lines 1020–1072.

The total allocated amount is the absolute value of the sum of signed allocations, and its company equivalent is the absolute value of summed calculated company allocations. Retain individual signed rows, especially when credit invoices participate, rather than storing only the absolute aggregate. The base unallocated amount is the remaining party amount after relevant ordinary deductions and included taxes, converted back through the party-side rate. It is reset to zero before recalculation and only populated when the branch's available amount exceeds allocated amount. Evidence: source-artifact-151c197ffc6d8c7575df lines 1074–1131.

Let party company amount be allocated company amount plus unallocated amount multiplied by party-side rate. Receipt difference is party company amount less company received amount plus signed included taxes, less deductions. Payment difference is company paid amount less party company amount less included taxes, less deductions. Transfer difference is company paid less company received less included taxes, less deductions. Submission requires the final rounded difference to equal zero. A saved draft is not proof of a balanced submitted payment. Evidence: source-artifact-151c197ffc6d8c7575df lines 1194–1235; source-artifact-151c197ffc6d8c7575df lines 204–215.

Exchange or bank-charge adjustment equals company paid less company received; for internal transfers it also subtracts ordinary nonexchange deductions already supplied. Recalculation consolidates this into one exchange-adjustment row, removing duplicate generated rows and removing the row when the amount becomes zero. For a single-currency discrepancy, a configured bank-charge account is preferred; a cross-currency discrepancy uses exchange gain or loss. Account and cost-center defaults are required before submission. Evidence: source-artifact-151c197ffc6d8c7575df lines 1133–1192.

Source test expectation: transfer 100 foreign units at company rate 50 from one bank account, receive 4500.00 company units into another, and explicitly record 100.00 bank charge. Company paid is 5000.00. The automatic remaining exchange loss is 400.00. Final postings are source bank credit 5000.00, destination bank debit 4500.00, bank-charge debit 100.00 and exchange-loss debit 400.00; difference is zero. A single 500.00 bank-charge posting would lose the distinct exchange loss. Evidence: source-artifact-34f5d9a7e966584075b4 lines 888–944.

## Reference validation and foreign settlement

Reference discovery is filtered by company, party kind, party identity, party account, payment direction and supported document kinds. Each reference stores current outstanding, invoice total, allocated amount, optional payment term and historical exchange rate. Allocation validation re-fetches current references. A missing reference can mean the invoice was already fully paid; a changed partial balance is a stale-data error rather than permission to reuse the previous amount. Term-based allocation requires a named term and checks term outstanding as well as total outstanding. Negative references apply a mirrored boundary. Evidence: source-artifact-151c197ffc6d8c7575df lines 429–510.

Foreign settlement exchange difference is the allocated amount at payment-side rate less the same allocated amount at reference rate, with both company values rounded first. The payment journal uses the payment-side rate; a linked exchange journal reconciles the reference's historical amount. Keep this realized exchange difference distinct from the bank-transfer rate discrepancy and from period-end unrealized revaluation. Evidence: source-artifact-151c197ffc6d8c7575df lines 1033–1072; source-artifact-34f5d9a7e966584075b4 lines 1078–1112.

Reconciliation after posting compares payment and invoice exchange rates only when the party account currency differs from company currency and both rates are relevant. A payable-party reverse allocation against a payment or journal reverses the normal difference sign. For ordinary customer reconciliation, a positive difference denotes gain; for supplier reconciliation, a negative difference denotes gain. The exchange posting date defaults to payment date; configuration can instead select invoice date or reconciliation date for nonadvance allocations. Evidence: source-artifact-a36a66a3f4375c8d5d7d lines 427–462; source-artifact-a36a66a3f4375c8d5d7d lines 482–534.

## Allocation ordering and state changes

The allocation builder iterates payments and then invoices in the order supplied. Each pair allocates the smaller of available payment and invoice outstanding, subtracts it from both sides, and continues until one is exhausted. It is not inherently an oldest-invoice algorithm; any oldest-first ordering must be applied by the caller or discovery query. Zero allocations are omitted. A replacement must return the proposed allocation matrix for review before posting, preserve the user-selected order, and then revalidate available amounts during the mutation. Evidence: source-artifact-a36a66a3f4375c8d5d7d lines 482–534.

Derived fixture: payments 300.00 and 250.00 allocated to invoices 200.00 and 400.00 in that order produce allocations 200.00 from first payment to first invoice, 100.00 from first payment to second, and 250.00 from second payment to second. Both payments are exhausted and the second invoice retains 50.00 outstanding. Changing order can change which invoice remains outstanding while total allocation stays 550.00.

The reviewed reconciliation validation rejects a row greater than its remaining payment amount. Its invoice comparison includes a tolerance of 0.009, distinct from payment-entry validation and ledger balancing allowances. It requires at least one allocation. Do not use that local tolerance as a universal monetary epsilon. Concurrent allocation behaviour requires execution testing: the review confirms latest-data checks in payment entry, but it does not establish serializable isolation for every reconciliation path. Evidence: source-artifact-a36a66a3f4375c8d5d7d lines 748–777.

## Bank-statement reconciliation

Statement currency must equal the linked financial bank-account currency. Duplicate voucher kind and voucher identity pairs in one statement transaction are rejected. Statement allocated amount is the sum of linked allocation rows. Statement remaining amount is absolute withdrawal less deposit, minus allocated amount, rounded to field precision. A submitted statement is unreconciled while the remaining amount is positive and reconciled when it is zero or negative. The negative condition is an observed status rule, not permission to bypass over-allocation validation. Evidence: source-artifact-93f4d60a990dafc3fca9 lines 60–116.

When adding matches, create zero-valued provisional links and let the allocation routine compute their amounts. For each candidate voucher, find its relevant bank ledger amount and amounts already allocated across statement records. Available amount is voucher amount less prior statement allocation. Negative available amount is rejected; zero available removes the redundant match and may update clearance; positive available receives the smaller of available amount and current statement remainder. Existing nonzero allocations are not automatically overwritten. A voucher is cleared when its full available amount is covered, using the applicable latest statement date. A bank-to-bank statement match updates the linked statement as well. Evidence: source-artifact-93f4d60a990dafc3fca9 lines 153–236.

Cancellation delinks the statement's payment references and updates status. Removing a previously saved link must also remove its clearance effect where appropriate, rather than leaving a payment appearing cleared with no supporting statement. Statement matching and unreconciliation therefore mutate reconciliation records and clearance metadata while retaining the originally posted money movement.

## Bank fee normalization

An included fee on a withdrawal cannot exceed the withdrawal, although equality is allowed. An included fee recorded alongside a deposit is not constrained to be below the deposit by that rule. A positive excluded fee is normalized before further processing: subtract it from a deposit, or add it to a withdrawal; add it to included fee and reset excluded fee to zero. A deposit cannot become negative through this conversion. Both deposit and withdrawal nonzero are rejected when applying an excluded fee. If both are initially zero, a positive excluded fee becomes a withdrawal. Evidence: source-artifact-93f4d60a990dafc3fca9 lines 329–362.

Source test expectations: deposit 100.00, existing included fee 2.00 and excluded fee 5.00 become deposit 95.00, included fee 7.00, excluded fee zero. Withdrawal 100.00 with the same fees becomes withdrawal 105.00 and included fee 7.00. Deposit 5.00 with excluded fee 5.00 becomes zero deposit and included fee 5.00. Deposit 10.00 with excluded fee 11.00 is rejected. These normalization rules should be tested before any automatic statement matching rule is applied. Evidence: source-artifact-d57965db23c9660fc330 lines 10–134.

## Foreign balance revaluation

Revaluation identifies eligible foreign-currency balance-sheet leaf accounts, excluding stock accounts in the default discovery. It groups balances by account and party. Company and account-currency amounts are summed from effective ledger entries through the selected date and rounded; balances within configured rounding allowance become zero. For a normal nonzero pair, implied old rate equals company balance divided by account balance; new company balance equals account balance multiplied by selected new rate. Revaluation adjustment equals rounded new company balance less rounded old company balance. The foreign amount remains unchanged. Evidence: source-artifact-48613d6d0d376eea99f3 lines 187–309.

Derived fixture: foreign receivable balance 100.00 with company carrying value 1800.00 revalued at 19 yields new company carrying value 1900.00 and unrealized gain 100.00. The revaluation debits the receivable company value and credits unrealized exchange gain while foreign receivable remains 100.00. A foreign liability has negative debit-minus-credit balance, so the sign naturally reverses. Revaluation is not a cash receipt and must not settle the invoice.

Zero-balance cleanup is a separate branch. If account-currency balance is zero but company balance remains, both target balances become zero and adjustment removes the company residual. If company balance is zero but account-currency balance remains, the calculation uses the last relevant ledger exchange rate for cleanup. Specialized journals and reversal links support the resulting adjustments. Those zero-sided journals explain why ordinary all-zero company-row rejection has a narrow exchange-journal exception. ## Acceptance boundary

Required acceptance compares bank balances, party settlement balances, invoice and term outstanding, unallocated advances, realized exchange, unrealized exchange, fees and statement clearance independently. Test two payments racing to settle the same outstanding amount; payment cancellation after matching; statement cancellation without payment cancellation; reverse allocation of a credit; partial statement coverage across dates; same-currency fee versus foreign exchange loss; and period-end revaluation reversal. Numerical formulas and source test expectations above are reviewed evidence. Concurrency, retry identity and external bank-import provider behaviour remain explicit verification obligations; their implementation cannot be inferred from the presence of a reconciliation screen.
