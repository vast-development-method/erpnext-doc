## Ordered commercial calculation

The order of arithmetic is part of the business contract. The current calculation validates document currency and conversion, calculates item rates and values, validates item tax templates, loads item-specific tax rates, initializes ordered tax rows, extracts tax-exclusive values from inclusive prices, sums net totals, calculates taxes, adjusts inclusive rounding differences, calculates grand totals and settlement rounding, and calculates total weight. It then applies document discount where relevant and recomputes the affected values before shipping, advance and outstanding calculations. Alternative quotation items are excluded from the set contributing to totals.

Maintain input list rate, margin kind and amount, discount percentage, per-unit discount, accepted selling or buying rate, commercial quantity, amount, net rate, net amount, company equivalents and rule identities. With a margin percentage, margin-inclusive rate equals list rate multiplied by one plus margin percentage divided by 100. With a margin amount, add that amount. A positive discount percentage is evaluated against the margin-inclusive rate and rounded to discount-field precision. Accepted unit rate is the margin-inclusive rate less per-unit discount, rounded to rate precision. Amount is accepted unit rate multiplied by quantity, rounded to amount precision.

A manually supplied rate can win over a computed discount when no applicable pricing rule forces the calculation. If the supplied rate exceeds list rate, represent the difference as an amount margin and clear discount. Otherwise represent list rate less supplied rate as discount amount, clear percentage and margin. A zero list rate clears margin and discount. A zero commercial quantity on a permitted value-only return creates negative unit rate as amount; a permitted value-only debit adjustment uses positive unit rate. These exceptions must be gated by the document's validation contract and do not permit zero-quantity stock movement.

Reviewed test expectation: list rate 1000.00 with a ten percent margin becomes 1100.00. A ten percent discount on that rate is 110.00, so final unit rate is 990.00. An amount discount of 110.00 after the same margin also yields 990.00. Applying discount to the original list rate would give the wrong result.

## Pricing rule selection

Rules can apply by item, item group, brand or transaction; buying and selling applicability are separate. Selection includes party and group conditions, company, date range, currency, price list, quantity and amount limits, optional expression conditions, variant context, and explicit priority. Item-level specificity is considered before group and brand unless priority or multiple-rule policy causes further candidates to participate. Conflicting equally applicable ordinary rules are rejected instead of choosing an arbitrary first record. An expression that fails evaluation is logged and does not pass as a valid match. The portable specification requires an equivalent declarative predicate contract; it does not require executing the source expression language.

Eligibility normally uses stock quantity and commercial list rate multiplied by commercial quantity. A rule expressed in a specific unit converts its quantity thresholds to stock quantity. Amount-threshold conversion also depends on rule unit, except matching transaction-unit item prices retain factor one. Minimum and maximum boundaries are inclusive, and a missing or zero maximum is unbounded. Mixed-item rules aggregate qualifying lines. Cumulative rules add prior eligible quantities and amounts. These features are different from a per-line volume discount and must retain their calculation scope.

When selecting one rule, the highest explicit priority wins after eligibility; currency-specific and price-list-specific filtering can further disambiguate. When several rules are explicitly allowed, they are applied in ascending priority sequence. A fixed-rate rule with matching currency replaces the list rate, scaled by transaction conversion when rule unit does not equal transaction unit. A zero fixed-rate rule does not replace an existing item price.

Multiple percentage discounts can be additive against the list-rate basis or sequential against the discounted rate. For sequential percentages, accumulated discount becomes previous discount plus the undiscounted percentage multiplied by the next percentage divided by 100. Reviewed test expectation: first discount 20 percent and second discount 10 percent applied to the remainder yield 28 percent total, not 30 percent. On list price 100.00 the resulting derived selling rate is 72.00.

Free-product rules create a separate item line with product identity, unit, conversion factor, quantity, rule identity, free-item marker and configured free-item rate, which can be zero. A recursive award uses eligible nonfree commercial quantity after a configured offset, multiplied by free quantity divided by repeat interval. If whole awards are required, floor the number of intervals first. Reviewed test expectations: one free item per two purchased within the rule range gives two free items for five purchased and three for seven; ten free per hundred purchased gives ten free for both 100 and 150. Removing eligibility removes the generated award. The free line still has fulfillment and inventory consequences even when its selling amount is zero.

## Tax data model

Each tax line retains order, tax account, charge kind, rate or fixed amount, previous-row reference when applicable, included-in-price flag, addition or deduction, valuation classification, cost center and dimensions, amount before and after document discount, company equivalents and cumulative total. Each item can override tax rate for a tax account or mark that tax inapplicable. The inapplicable marker differs from a zero rate because it suppresses applicability rather than simply multiplying by zero. Persist item-wise tax details linked to the actual document line, not only the product code: repeated product rows can have different rates, dates, discounts or quantities.

| Charge kind | Calculation for a particular item |
|---|---|
| Fixed actual document amount | Item net amount multiplied by document charge divided by document net total; residual goes to final item |
| Percentage of net amount | Item taxable net amount multiplied by percentage divided by 100 |
| Percentage of earlier tax amount | Referenced tax row's amount for this item multiplied by percentage divided by 100 |
| Percentage of earlier cumulative total | Referenced tax row's net-plus-tax cumulative amount for this item multiplied by percentage divided by 100 |
| Per commercial unit | Rate multiplied by commercial item quantity |
| Registered custom taxable base | Resolved item base multiplied by percentage divided by 100 |

Tax row order is semantically significant. Previous-row references address earlier calculation results for the same item. A valuation-only charge is excluded from cumulative amount due. A purchase deduction contributes a negative amount to the cumulative total. Item-specific rates override account-row rates.

## Inclusive prices and compound tax

Inclusive tax extraction represents each included charge as slope multiplied by unknown net amount plus a per-unit intercept. A net-percentage tax has slope equal to percentage divided by 100 and zero intercept. A per-unit charge has zero slope and its unit charge as intercept. A percentage of an earlier charge multiplies both that earlier slope and intercept. A percentage of an earlier cumulative total uses that cumulative net coefficient and cumulative intercept. A custom fixed taxable base becomes an intercept rather than a coefficient of the unknown selling net. Deducted included taxes negate their slope and intercept.

For one item, net amount before rounding equals printed amount less total intercept multiplied by quantity, divided by one plus total slope. Round the net amount to net precision, then calculate net unit rate from that rounded net divided by quantity. The unrounded net remains available for included net-percentage tax to avoid double rounding.

Derived compound fixture: printed amount 115.50 contains ten percent tax on net plus five percent tax on net including that first tax. First slope is 0.10; second slope is 0.05 multiplied by 1.10, giving 0.055. Net is 115.50 divided by 1.155, or 100.00. Tax amounts are 10.00 and 5.50. Treating both taxes as ordinary independent percentages would incorrectly produce net 100.43.

Source test expectation for custom base: printed amount 1000.00 contains a ten percent charge calculated on a separately resolved list-value base of 1200.00. The included charge is 120.00 and net is 880.00; grand total remains 1000.00. In an exclusive custom-base fixture, net 1000.00 with ten percent of base 1000.00 produces charge 100.00 and grand total 1100.00.

## Tax rounding and residuals

The system has a setting for item-row tax rounding. When enabled, each item's contribution is rounded before accumulation. Otherwise contributions accumulate at their intermediate precision before tax-row totals are rounded. Fixed actual charges track the difference between required document amount and allocated item shares; the final item absorbs the residual. This keeps an actual 10.00 charge equal to 10.00 when split across three equally weighted items.

Item-wise tax detail is reconciled to each tax row's final company amount, signed for deductions. The final detail row for the tax absorbs a difference rounded to five places when its magnitude is at most 0.50, or at most 1.00 for a zero-decimal tax currency. Larger differences are rejected unless a controlled internal validation bypass is active. The tolerance is not a general license to discard tax differences.

Inclusive-total reconciliation compares printed total plus nonincluded charges with calculated cumulative total, adjusting for document discount already applied. A difference no greater than five smallest tax decimal units can become a grand-total difference. Larger differences are not silently absorbed by this step. Tax totals are rounded to field precision; regionally designated tax accounts can first round to integer values.

## Document discount

A percentage document discount is converted to an amount using the selected net-total or grand-total basis. Discount may not exceed the total before that discount, with signed validation for returns and consideration of previously returned discount. Ordinary document discount is distributed across eligible item net amounts in proportion to their contribution to the eligible basis, and the affected taxes are recomputed. Maintain running unrounded expected net total and rounded actual net total; apply their rounded difference to the current item's net amount and inversely to its distributed discount, so cumulative rounding stays reconciled.

A grand-total discount excludes fixed actual charges and per-unit charges from the discountable basis, including the fixed-charge portion of dependent taxes. Percentage taxes on the remaining discountable base reduce through recomputation. A cash or nontrade grand-total discount is a different path: it subtracts from grand total and company grand total after normal tax calculation without redistributing item net amounts or taxes.

Derived trade-discount fixture: net 100.00, percentage tax ten percent and fixed freight 10.00 give grand total 120.00. A grand-total discount amount 11.00 uses eligible basis 110.00, excluding fixed freight. Net becomes 90.00, percentage tax 9.00 and freight stays 10.00, yielding 109.00. A cash-discount policy can also yield 109.00 due while retaining original net 100.00 and tax 10.00 and separately accounting for the 11.00 discount. Identical money due does not establish identical accounting.

## Withholding thresholds and historical adjustments

Applicable withholding rules are effective-date, category, withholding-group and company scoped. Threshold evaluation follows this decision order: an explicit threshold override succeeds; transaction-only mode compares current taxable amount to single threshold; a zero cumulative threshold succeeds; excess-only taxation proceeds to exemption handling; otherwise inspect submitted historical entries in the category's effective interval. Historical scope includes company and party kind, and uses shared tax identity where present, otherwise party identity. A positive settled historical taxable amount keeps the threshold crossed. Otherwise remaining threshold equals cumulative threshold less under-withheld historical taxable amount, limited by single threshold unless transaction threshold is disabled. Equality crosses the threshold.

Below-threshold taxable events are still persisted with zero withholding and without a withholding-document link, so the next event can account for accumulated taxable value. Excess-only taxation first creates an exemption entry for the minimum of unused threshold and current taxable amount. Remaining taxable value proceeds through historical under-withheld and over-withheld adjustment, lower-deduction certificate utilization where applicable, and normal withholding. Taxable event and withholding event identities remain separate because the withholding can occur later than the original taxable event.

Derived threshold fixture with no tax identity shared across parties: cumulative threshold 1000.00, single threshold disabled, prior under-withheld taxable amount 700.00 and current taxable amount 300.00 reach threshold exactly. A current amount 299.99 does not cross. With excess-only policy, previously consumed exemption 700.00 and current amount 500.00 create exemption 300.00 and remaining taxable basis 200.00. Historical recovery and certificate allocations require additional numerical fixtures beyond these threshold decisions.

## Portable extension and acceptance boundary

Taxable-base resolvers receive the relevant item and tax context and return an amount used by the common calculation. Regional posting extensions can add tax consequences after standard invoice composition and before final posting. A replacement should specify deterministic inputs and outputs, permitted fields, error behaviour and review authority for these extension points. It must not copy an implementation-language expression engine merely to preserve extensibility.

Acceptance must compare intermediate rate, net amount, item tax detail, document tax, company tax, grand total, rounded total, discount distribution and final ledger composition. Required fixtures include half-even versus half-away ties; zero-decimal currencies; three-item fixed-charge allocation; inapplicable versus zero tax; duplicate product lines; two compound included taxes; included per-unit tax; custom fixed base; trade versus cash discount; conflicting pricing rules; rule priority; free packs; inclusive quantity boundaries; and withholding equality. Local statutory rates, regional exemptions and certificates require their own sourced specifications; a generic tax engine does not by itself prove jurisdiction compliance.

## Withholding allocation after the threshold decision

Withholding processing distinguishes the taxable document from the document that actually retained tax. Current taxable value is placed first in the queue of under-withheld events, followed by eligible historical under-withheld values. Existing over-withheld events supply already retained amounts. An over-withheld payment or journal participates only when linked to the current document. For linked payments, its usable withholding is scaled by linked payment allocation divided by taxable amount less withholding amount, capped at one; a zero denominator is skipped. This is the observed scaling rule, and it must not be replaced by an assumed gross-amount proportion without a policy change.

Match the first under-withheld event with the first eligible over-withheld event. Compute tax on the under-withheld value at the over-withheld event's rate, capped by that event's available withheld amount and rounded to withholding precision. For a nonzero rate, matched taxable value equals matched tax multiplied by 100 divided by that rate, rounded again. For a zero rate, match the smaller taxable value directly. Preserve the original taxable identity and the prior withholding identity, reduce both queues by the matched values, and continue until one is exhausted. Matching prior withholding does not deduct the same money from the current supplier again.

After matching, a lower-deduction certificate consumes up to its remaining taxable allowance at the certificate rate. The normal category rate then applies to the remaining taxable queue. Preserve certificate identity, allocated taxable amount, certificate rate, normal rate, effective interval and company account. Monetary limits and taxable-value limits are different: an allowance of 400.00 taxable at two percent authorizes 8.00 tax, not 400.00 tax.

Derived fixtures use threshold already crossed, two decimal places, no tax-round-to-integer option and no shared-party tax identity. A current 1000.00 taxable amount with 400.00 certificate allowance at two percent and ordinary rate ten percent yields 8.00 certificate withholding plus 60.00 ordinary withholding, totaling 68.00. A current 1000.00 taxable amount with eligible prior withholding 30.00 at ten percent first matches 300.00 taxable value to that prior event and withholds another 70.00 on the remaining 700.00. If a cumulative threshold is first crossed with historical untaxed value 700.00 and current value 300.00, ordinary ten percent withholding can collect 100.00 in the current event while retaining the taxable identity of each portion. These cases are in the local accounting acceptance catalog.

Category withholding is aggregated by company tax account. The generated invoice tax line is a fixed actual charge, total-only, with addition-or-deduction selected by the current withholding amount's sign. The item-level breakup is then regenerated using each eligible item's taxable share and the category amount. Exemption amounts are proportionally removed from the item's effective base. Remove zero generated withholding tax rows before recalculating invoice totals. A category amount recovered for a historical taxable event therefore still needs the historical identity even when its current document tax row is aggregated into one account total.

## Tax calculation extension contract

A custom taxable-base resolver receives the approved item identity, ordered tax row, item quantity and unit conversion, list and accepted prices, currency, company, relevant dates and explicitly permitted item attributes. It returns a base amount in the declared calculation currency or a structured error. It must declare whether the base is a fixed amount or proportional to the unknown tax-exclusive net amount so included-price extraction can select an intercept or coefficient correctly. Do not allow a resolver to change quantities, settlement allocations or ledger history as a side effect of asking for a taxable base.

The purity and explicit currency declaration above are replacement interface requirements. The observed extensibility establishes a callback for resolving the base; it does not itself prove callback isolation or purity. Retain evaluated base, selected rule identity and relevant effective policy with the approved transaction so an auditor can recompute the tax after the rule configuration changes.

## Exact rounding examples

A fixed 10.00 charge distributed over three equal net lines at two places becomes 3.33, 3.33 and 3.34; the total remains 10.00. With three taxable lines of 0.05 and a ten percent rate, item-row half-away rounding yields 0.01 per line and 0.03 total, whereas accumulation before rounding gives 0.015 and final 0.02. Under half-even item rounding, each 0.005 becomes 0.00. Thus both rounding method and the stage at which rounding occurs belong in the calculation profile.

For six units with printed total 138.00 including a per-unit charge 2.00 and a ten percent charge on the net plus that per-unit charge, the intercept is 2.20 per unit and slope is 0.10. Tax-exclusive net is (138.00 − 6 × 2.20) ÷ 1.10 = 113.454545…, rounded to 113.45. The per-unit charge is 12.00 and the dependent percentage contribution before final rounding is 12.545454… . The rounded dependent charge is 12.55 and the final total is 138.00. Calculating ten percent only on the net would omit the tax-on-per-unit component.
