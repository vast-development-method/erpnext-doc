# Financial accounting

The linked definitions contain the complete field, relationship, choice, default and permission catalogs. This chapter supplies their financial meaning, calculations, lifecycle decisions and accounting consequences. [Financial calculations and numerical fixtures](../../schemas/mathematics/financial-calculations.json) provide machine-readable companions to the equations and examples below. No external source files are needed to follow these contracts.

## Purpose and interpretation

Financial accounting converts approved business events into company financial balances while retaining the event, counterparty, dimensions, and monetary measurements that explain every balance. A replacement must reproduce the posting decisions and observable adjustments described here. The records are business records; the design does not require a particular database engine, execution language, or deployment topology. Numerical acceptance examples state inputs and expected results directly; they are requirements for the replacement and do not claim that a running reference application was tested. Derived examples are arithmetic fixtures for the stated rules.

The system has more than one financial history. The general ledger measures debits and credits by account. The party settlement ledger measures invoice and payment allocation. The inventory ledger measures quantities and valuation movements. Asset schedules measure depreciation events by financial book. An invoice's outstanding amount cannot safely be reconstructed from only the general ledger because a later allocation can update the settlement ledger without retagging the original general ledger rows. These histories must remain reconcilable without erasing their distinct purposes.

## Persistent model and identity

| Record | Required business identity and retained facts | Relationships and consequences |
|---|---|---|
| Company | Identity, company currency, reporting currency, financial calendar, posting defaults, frozen-through date | Owns accounts, cost centers, financial books and posting policy |
| Account | Identity, company, parent, grouping flag, root classification, reporting classification, account type, account currency, status | Group accounts aggregate; only eligible leaf accounts accept ordinary transactions |
| Accounting dimension | Identity, referenced classification, company-specific requirement, default, allowed values, optional balancing account | Extends journal segmentation without changing the meaning of debit and credit |
| General ledger entry | Identity, voucher type and identity, voucher line, posting date, financial year, company, account, counterparty, due date, dimensions, currency amounts, opening indicator, cancellation indicator | Explains company balances and links to the originating transaction |
| Party settlement entry | Identity, company, counterparty and type, receivable or payable account, originating voucher, settled-against voucher, amounts, dimensions, delinked indicator | Determines outstanding amounts and later reconciliations |
| Journal | Identity, type, posting date, company, financial book, explanation, ordered journal lines, referenced invoices, payments or advances | General purpose controlled posting document |
| Period closing | Identity, company, financial year, interval, closing account, processing state | Generates closing postings and closing balance records |
| Budget | Identity, company, interval, account, dimension, distribution, enforcement choices | Rejects or warns on commitments and actual expense |

A counterparty link consists of both party kind and party identity. Invoice identity consists of document kind and document identity; source line identity is separately retained. Two companies can use similarly named accounts while those accounts remain different financial entities. Do not merge transactions using account display name alone.

## Money, precision, and currencies

Every general ledger line supports company currency, account currency, and transaction currency debit and credit values. It additionally calculates reporting-currency values. Preserve all four measurement contexts and their rates. The company value supports the balanced journal; account value supports a foreign-denominated balance; transaction value explains the source commercial document; reporting value supports an additional reporting presentation. They are related but are not interchangeable columns.

For normal invoice conversion, company amount equals the already field-rounded transaction amount multiplied by the transaction-to-company rate, rounded to the corresponding company field precision. Summing individually converted rows can differ from converting the summed transaction total; the difference is posted explicitly to a rounding account. Reporting values use the company-to-reporting rate at transaction date when supplied, otherwise posting date. Missing reporting conversion rejects the entry.

The precision of a field is part of its contract. A monetary field, rate, conversion factor, valuation rate, and percentage need not share precision. The standard item calculation deliberately avoids applying normal field rounding to valuation rate, incoming rate, sales incoming rate, and unit conversion factor. A decimal implementation should reproduce the declared rounding points, rather than rounding every intermediate number to two places. Exact decimal representation is a proposed implementation quality choice; compatibility is defined by expected results, not by the arithmetic representation used.

Three active selectable tie policies must be retained: half to even, half away from zero, and scale-sensitive ties. The scale-sensitive policy is the runtime fallback when no method is specified: it rounds the scaled intermediate to eight decimal places; at integer precision a half tie goes to the even integer, while at nonzero precision a half tie goes toward positive infinity. The source setting's historical label does not make this active behaviour a migration feature. At two places, 2.345 becomes 2.34 under half to even and 2.35 under both other policies; negative 2.345 becomes negative 2.34 under half to even and scale-sensitive ties, and negative 2.35 under half away from zero. Currency settlement rounding is a separate operation: with a configured smallest cash fraction, take the remainder; a remainder strictly above half the fraction rounds upward, while a tie rounds downward to the lower multiple. Without a fraction, the configured integer rounding applies. This means currency rounding must not silently reuse the generic decimal tie rule.

## Posting pipeline and balancing

The posting request carries the entire proposed entry set for one business event. Ordinary processing validates the current budget rules, inserts required dimension offsets, validates the accounting period and disabled accounts, allocates cost centers, merges eligible entries, normalizes negative sides, creates party settlement entries for applicable account movements, and persists validated ledger rows. A nonempty result with only one ordinary ledger row is rejected. Period closing deliberately uses different settlement and allocation paths.

Merging preserves account, cost center, party kind and identity, voucher line, settled-against document kind and identity, project, financial book, voucher identity, advance kind and identity, and every configured accounting dimension. A row may prohibit merging. Amounts in all three primary currency contexts are added independently. Zero company debit and credit rows disappear after precision rounding except for the specialized exchange gain or loss journal. A rebuild that combines all rows for one account would destroy invoice allocations and dimensional evidence.

Negative debit transfers its absolute value to credit; negative credit transfers its absolute value to debit. If net-value posting is requested and both sides are positive, retain only the difference on the greater side. The operation applies independently to company, account, and transaction values. Equal negative debit and credit values are first made positive, a specific observed edge case that must have its own acceptance fixture if exposed by an interface.

Let the company imbalance be the rounded sum of individually rounded debits less individually rounded credits. For journals and payments the allowance is five smallest decimal units, meaning 0.05 at two places. Other voucher kinds use an allowance of 0.50. An imbalance above the allowance rejects an ordinary posting. A nonzero imbalance at least one smallest decimal unit but within allowance produces a balancing rounding entry. Excess debits create a rounding credit; excess credits create a rounding debit. The specialized exchange gain or loss journal has an explicit exception to the ordinary imbalance rejection. This is observed behaviour, not a recommendation to accept arbitrary imbalance.

Derived example: proposed debit 100.00 and credit 99.98 at two places produce a rounding credit of 0.02. A payment with debit 100.00 and credit 99.94 exceeds the 0.05 allowance and is rejected. A sales invoice with the same 0.06 imbalance is within its 0.50 allowance and creates a rounding entry. The distinct allowances are contractual and should not be collapsed into one constant.

## Validation and posting authority

Ordinary lines require company, account, voucher kind, and voucher identity. The account must be a non-group account, active, and owned by the posting company. A cost center must belong to the same company and ordinarily cannot be a group. Profit and loss account entries require a cost center. Required accounting dimensions can differ between profit and loss accounts and balance sheet accounts. Receivable and payable entries require the corresponding party context unless a controlled internal operation explicitly suppresses that requirement. Account currency must match the account definition, and party currency compatibility is separately checked. Opening entries cannot post to profit and loss accounts.

Frozen-through dates, account freezing, closed accounting periods, closed financial periods, and restricted dimension values are distinct gates. A user allowed to read or edit an invoice is not automatically allowed to bypass these gates. The posting engine is an internal service boundary: it may create generated ledger records under elevated internal authority only after the originating transaction's permissions and business validations have succeeded. A rebuild must not expose a public unrestricted ledger insertion command.

## Cost center allocation and balancing dimensions

Select the latest submitted cost-center allocation whose effective date is on or before posting date and whose company and main cost center match the line. Each child receives each primary amount multiplied by its percentage divided by 100 and rounded to ledger precision. The rounding account is assigned to the first allocation child instead of being split. Budget validation occurs against the main cost center before distribution. Derived example: expense 100.00 split 60 and 40 percent produces child expenses 60.00 and 40.00; a three-way equal split can require a separate journal rounding adjustment.

An accounting dimension configured for automatic balancing contributes offsets only when the proposed journal contains more than one value for that dimension. If several such dimensions qualify, each receives a share of the reversed company debit or credit divided by the number of qualifying dimensions. Offset rows clear party and settled-against links and use the configured balancing account. The reviewed routine places company-derived amounts into account-currency columns; foreign-currency offset-account behaviour needs a dedicated execution fixture before a rebuild treats such a combination as supported.

## Cancellation and correction

Cancellation creates reversing entries by swapping debit and credit in company, account and transaction currencies. It also reverses settlement effects and checks frozen and closed periods. The source locks the original matching ledger rows while collecting the reversal set. Under the history-preserving policy, the originals remain effective history and reversals use an explicitly supplied reversal posting date or the current date. Under the alternative active policy, originals and their reversals are marked cancelled and ordinary balances exclude them. These are active policy alternatives; neither should be confused with obsolete data migration. For a new installation, choosing only the history-preserving policy is a proposed scope choice and must be declared.

The reversal must preserve original company, account, party, dimensions, financial book, source references, and values. Separate advance-account reconciliation can reverse only lines matching a specific reference and voucher-line identity. A cancellation must not recompute its financial amount from today's item price or exchange rate. Reporting-currency recomputation during reversal needs a dedicated fixture because reporting values are populated again by the record validation path.

Reporting values are recalculated from the reversed company amounts using transaction date when the copied original has one, otherwise the reversal posting date. The rate lookup must succeed even when the transaction is a cancellation. Thus preservation of company, account and transaction amounts does not imply preservation of reporting amounts when the effective rate date changes. For example, an original company debit 100.00 reported at 1.20 produces reporting debit 120.00. A reversal with no original transaction date and a reversal-date reporting rate of 1.30 produces reporting credit 130.00, while company debit and credit still net to zero. Whether that reporting residual is subsequently revalued is a separate reporting policy; it is not another customer claim.

An individual ledger entry cannot be cancelled through a public document command. Cancellation must originate from the owning transaction and select its complete eligible reversal set. A restricted advance-allocation reversal additionally matches company, account, party kind, party identity, original voucher, settled-against voucher and voucher-line identity. Using only the invoice identity would reverse unrelated applications of the same advance.

## Financial invariants and command outcomes

Let an effective entry mean an entry included by the selected cancellation policy. For an account and reporting interval, the closing company balance is its opening balance plus the sum of effective debits less effective credits. A positive result is a debit balance; a negative result is a credit balance. Income and liability reports may reverse that display sign without changing the stored entry. Account currency and reporting currency balances are accumulated from their own stored amount pairs rather than reconstructed using a current rate.

\[
\operatorname{ClosingBalance}=\operatorname{OpeningBalance}+\sum_{\text{effective entries}}(\operatorname{Debit}-\operatorname{Credit}).
\]

For posting precision \(p\), define the smallest unit as \(10^{-p}\), and the imbalance as the rounded sum of individually rounded debit-minus-credit values. Ordinary journals and payments permit an absolute imbalance at most \(5\times10^{-p}\); other ordinary vouchers permit at most 0.50. This allowance determines whether an explicit rounding entry can repair the event. It does not authorize leaving a nonzero repairable imbalance hidden in balances.

A rounding entry uses the company rounding account and cost center, clears party and settlement references, and stores company and transaction differences separately. If any input is an opening entry, the separate opening-rounding account is required and the rounding row is also an opening entry. An already supplied rounding-account row is adjusted rather than blindly adding another one. Dimension values are copied from the originating voucher when that voucher carries every configured dimension; required missing values still pass through ordinary dimension validation.

The posting command must return the owning document identity and resulting lifecycle state, generated general-ledger identities, generated settlement identities where applicable, and any rounding amount. Failure must identify the failed condition and expose no partial effective event. The same requirement applies when settlement entries are prepared before the final general-ledger validation. A caller cannot infer success from an intermediate identifier alone. Repeating a successful submit against the same already submitted document must not create a second financial event; a correction requires the explicit reversal or amendment operation.

For an account configured to retain a debit balance, a negative effective debit-minus-credit balance is invalid; for an account configured to retain a credit balance, a positive balance is invalid. This is a separate account constraint from balancing the event as a whole. Controlled advance adjustments have an explicit bypass path and must retain their adjustment identity and authority.

The record shapes for these outcomes are [general ledger entry](../../schemas/data/record-types/general_ledger_entry.json), [party settlement entry](../../schemas/data/record-types/payment_ledger_entry.json), [journal](../../schemas/data/record-types/journal_entry.json), and [journal line](../../schemas/data/record-types/journal_entry_account.json). The relationships distinguish the event owner, the original event line, and the claim being settled.

## Closing, budgets, and consolidated reporting

Current closing processing partitions calculation work but retains a single business interval and completion state. Each slice groups effective entries by account and all dimensions, including cost center, financial book and project. Closing reverses the income and expense net balances and transfers the net result to the designated liability or equity account, which must use company currency. Balance-sheet closing values are retained separately. Completion is recorded only after closing entries are written. A submitted closing document whose processing is still in progress must not be reported as completed.

Closing starts on the day after the last closed interval, or financial-year start. Its end cannot precede its start or exceed the financial-year end. If a prior financial year exists with effective postings and no closing record, the reviewed check blocks the new close. A later closing blocks creating or cancelling an earlier close. When perpetual inventory is enabled and at least one noncancelled stock movement exists on or before the close date, the inventory asset ledger balance must equal inventory value after both are rounded to ledger debit precision; a completed whole-company stock closing for the same date must exist and must not predate later changes to relevant stock history. Companies failing either prerequisite do not enter this stock-closing gate.

The current budget controller groups by dimension kind, dimension value and account. It measures outstanding purchase requests, unbilled purchase-order amounts, and actual expense, then applies independent annual and accumulated-period controls and optionally their cumulative exposure. Actual expense is effective ledger debit less credit. Outstanding order amount is ordered line amount less billed amount. Requested amount is remaining stock quantity multiplied by request rate. A designated exception approver turns a configured stop into a warning. Multiple matching budgets and cross-financial-year entry sets are explicit open questions in the reviewed source and are recorded in the review catalog.

Consolidated reporting traverses the company hierarchy and aligns accounts by account number with account name, or account name alone. It accumulates descendant-company movements when that option is selected, translates values where required, and rolls child accounts into parent accounts. This inspected routine establishes aggregation, not automatic elimination of intercompany profit, balances, or transactions. Such elimination must not be promised without a separately verified contract.

## Acceptance contract

1. A rejected company, currency, dimension, or period check leaves no effective ledger or settlement changes.
2. Two invoice lines using the same account but different source-line or settlement references remain distinguishable after posting.
3. The rounding fixtures above produce the stated acceptance or rejection, with visible rounding-account consequences.
4. Reversing a posted transaction nets its financial effects to zero under the chosen cancellation policy while retaining audit history.
5. A stock closing invalidated by a later backdated stock change cannot support financial close.
6. Closing revenue 1000.00 and expense 700.00 debits revenue 1000.00, credits expense 700.00, and credits the closing account 300.00, with corresponding dimensions retained.
7. A later payment allocation changes invoice outstanding without changing the total company cash already recorded.
8. Changing rate precision or rounding policy is treated as a change to calculation behaviour and reruns the numerical acceptance fixtures.
