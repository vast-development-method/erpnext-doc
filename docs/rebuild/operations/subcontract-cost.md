# Subcontract receipt, secondary output and return cost

The supplier holds company-owned inputs at a designated supplier warehouse. Sending those inputs moves custody. Finishing the job consumes the applicable materials, values output and records the service component. Keep the supplier's service purchase-order row, subcontract order row, finished receipt row, source raw-material rows, secondary recipe row, and downstream service receipt as separate references. Identical item codes may legitimately occur in different finished or secondary roles.

## Price one finished row

1. Retain accepted, rejected and process-loss quantities. Their sum is the costing quantity. Where BOM consumption validation applies, multiply recipe material-per-unit by costing quantity and conversion factor. Aggregate repeated components, compare the result to actual consumed quantity at quantity precision, and reject a shortage. BOMs permitting alternative items are skipped by this particular material-by-code check. Returns follow actual receipt consumption. Under transfer-based backflush the configuration may disable this lower-bound check.
2. Sum each consumed quantity × its valuation rate with the same finished-row reference. Add the row's service fee, allocated additional charges and landed cost in company currency. Generate secondary receipts with a reference to the parent finished row and recipe output row.
3. Manual secondary output starts at base recipe cost / specified secondary quantity. For a valuation-rate secondary use its receipt warehouse's rate and apply the documented fallback when unavailable. Recalculate the value on warehouse changes. Deduct these own-cost secondary values from gross cost before applying percentage shares.
4. Each percentage product takes its configured percentage of that same residual pool. The finished good takes its BOM allocation percentage of the residual. Divide value by accepted output quantity for the resulting accepted rate in the simple all-accepted case. On returns, use each secondary's rate from the specific original receipt. A reprice in today's warehouse must not change a historical return.

For ten accepted finished units, materials cost 70, service costs 20 and other charge 10. One manual by-product worth 20 leaves 80. A percentage co-product taking 25% of this base receives 20 and the finished product's 75% share is 60, i.e. 6 per unit. In a perpetual-inventory example with one co-product and one by-product warehouse, debit finished stock 60, by-product stock 20 and co-product stock 20, and credit supplier-held material stock 70, service receipt clearing 20 and the designated charge account 10. This example assumes all items are received, configured allocations total 100%, no taxes and no price or currency variances. ## Return accepted or rejected output

A linked return uses the same supplier warehouse as its original. Preserve the original finished row, original secondary row, original accepted/rejected location and rate, and quantities on prior effective returns. The rejected-warehouse return path takes no service cost or secondary product for a rejected unit. Moving the return to the accepted location changes the applicable branch. Secondary rows require a nonzero accepted quantity and finished-row reference and disallow rejected quantity.

On an accepted return, let *R* be original costing quantity, *A* its accepted quantity, *q* the signed accepted return, and *c* the original additional cost per costing unit. The signed additional cost is *q × c × R / A*. For 8 accepted and 2 rejected out of a costing quantity of 10 and freight 100, returning 4 accepted units returns 50 freight; returning all 8 returns 100. A rejected-warehouse return contributes zero to that allocated freight. Reconstruct original expense-account, currency and exchange-rate charge rows in proportion to returned allocated cost. If the total net charge is zero, use the ratio of returned allocation basis to original basis. A full accepted return of +100 freight and −100 rebate produces −100 freight and +100 rebate. Both lines must survive even though their sum is zero.

For each secondary quantity use the rounded cumulative entitlement after the next return minus the rounded entitlement before it. For six finished units originally accompanied by two indivisible secondary units, returning two finished units three times produces secondary return magnitudes 1, 0, 1. The final finished return also exhausts the remaining actual secondary quantities by original source row, including any rounding residual. An intervening cancelled return must release its entitlement. ## Generate the service purchase receipt

Generating a service purchase receipt requires a submitted subcontract receipt. Refuse a second non-return, non-cancelled service receipt against it, including an existing draft. Multiple purchase orders can contribute only if one receipt can express their currency, non-withholding tax sequence and discount terms. Compare charge row dependencies, rate, inclusion flag, account, category, add/deduct direction, cost centre, project and configured dimensions. Ignore stand-alone actual charge rows for comparing rate-based terms unless another tax depends on a previous row. Shared percentage discounts require matching basis and percentage. A fixed header discount belongs to its order and prevents combining separately identified orders. When combining actual charge amounts, match identical accounting keys by occurrence; two similar charges in one order must not both collapse into the first matching row of the other.

Submission updates order progress and consumption, physical reservations and warehouse ledger, checks outward batch availability, posts GL, triggers future revaluation, updates receipt status and the linked job card, and optionally creates the service purchase receipt. On cancelling a receipt verify its order remains actionable, reverse linked progress, stock and financial effects, update reservations and job-card quantities, and remove qualifying automatically created batches. Returned stock, cancellation and service receipt creation are three different commands.
