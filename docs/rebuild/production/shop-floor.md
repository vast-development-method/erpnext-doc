# Shop-floor execution and material reservation handoff

## Allocate finite capacity and record actual machine time

A work order operation owns a sequence, output, planned minutes and hourly rate. Its job cards distinguish planned slots, employee time logs, sub-operations, required materials, completed quantity, pending quantity and process loss. A workstation has concurrent capacity, enabled operating windows, a holiday list and an hourly cost. Retain card ID, operation row ID and employee ID when measuring a time interval.

| Decision | Observable rule |
|---|---|
| Schedule a card | Check existing actual logs on effective cards and applicable scheduled intervals on unstarted draft cards. Overlapping records for one card can occupy one capacity slot. Sort by start time and reuse a slot whose previous end is at or before the new start. A workstation type can be satisfied by an enabled workstation with capacity. |
| Align a start | After a conflict, advance past the chosen conflicting interval plus the configured minutes between operations. Recheck conflicts after moving the start into working hours. Advance over holidays unless production on holidays is allowed. When overtime is disabled split work across enabled shifts, sorted by start. No operating windows or enabled overtime allow the unrestricted-hours branch. |
| Admit an employee interval | End earlier than start fails. For completed intervals, overlap is strict: start < other end AND end > other start. Check same card and other effective cards for this employee, including other machines. Disclose another card's identity only with read access to it. |
| Charge machine time | Union overlapping or touching intervals on the card. With a start and recorded duration but missing end, derive end from start plus duration. If the row has only a duration, add it directly. Labour hours summed across employees and machine minutes can consequently differ. |
| Start or complete from a workstation | Require the matching open card and write access to start, submit access to complete. Completing closes every open time log at the supplied time, places the reported completed quantity only on the first open log, saves and submits. A card from another workstation cannot be completed through this command. |

For a shift 08:00–12:00 and 13:00–17:00, a 90-minute job starting 11:30 uses 30 minutes before lunch and 60 after, finishing 14:00. If two employees work on one card from 08:00–09:00 and 08:30–09:30, the card consumes 90 workstation minutes, while employee time logs total 120. With an hourly rate of 48 the job costs 72. Simultaneous employees produce one reported completion quantity at workstation close. An overlap by the *same* employee is independently disallowed even when machine capacity is two. ## Quantity through sequential operations

Ordinary job-card submission requires a running eligible work order, a card off hold, time logs, and any applicable inspection/transfer checks. A setting can require both timestamp ends for all logs. Subcontracted cards tracking semi-finished goods bypass this time-log check. The ordinary card's completed, lost and pending quantities must add to its assigned quantity at the configured precision. Pending quantity belongs to later work. With sub-operations, overall completion is the minimum of sub-operation completions. While a sub-operation remains unfinished, retain the booked loss. After applicable completions derive loss as max(0, assigned − completed − pending).

Allocate a work-order input across split cards from rounded cumulative entitlement less material already assigned on the operation's other cards. If 1.00 kg is required for three outputs, three equal one-unit cards at two decimal places get 0.33, 0.34 and 0.33 kg. Rounding each card independently would leave 0.01 kg unallocated.

Ordinary sequenced work cannot consume more than the least completed quantity of preceding required operations. Compare earlier submitted completions and losses on this operation plus the current card's completion and loss. If the limiting predecessor is completed and the overage fits entirely in the current card's process loss, reduce both this loss and assigned quantity by that excess. Otherwise reject. For a completed predecessor at 8, current assigned 10 with 7 completed and 3 proposed loss and no prior usage, accept after reducing loss to 1 and assigned quantity to 8. If 9 goods were reported complete, the excess cannot all be explained by loss. For tracked semi-finished production check the available manufactured quantities in the current operation's equivalent units; operation completion and stocked output are separate events. A cancellation must leave enough upstream completed quantity to support later operations already completed or lost. ## Cost regular and corrective operations

For each work-order operation, planned cost is planned minutes × hourly rate / 60. Use the effective workstation rate when the row's rate is missing/zero and a workstation is set. For a submitted work order with applicable regular cards, sum submitted non-corrective card minutes × the card's own hourly rate / 60 by work-order operation row. A known zero card cost is a real per-operation result; if no card aggregation exists use actual operation minutes × line rate / 60. Round per operation at the corresponding amount precision.

At header level, choose the sum of actual costs when nonzero or the sum of planned costs otherwise. Add additional operating cost and independently calculated corrective cost. Corrective cost sums submitted corrective cards by their own minutes and rates. Even if one operation has no reported activity, a nonzero total actual switches the entire header to actual; a replacement that substitutes planned cost separately on every idle operation will disagree. For planned costs [100, 50], actual [72, 0], additional 5 and corrective 10, total operating cost is 87. For [0, 0] actual under otherwise equal inputs it is 165. Financial posting of manufacturing stock is a subsequent movement, as described in the [manufacturing blueprint](../../domains/manufacturing-and-subcontracting.md). ## Reserve, hand off and release material identities

A stock reservation binds voucher type, voucher and exact row, item, stock UOM, warehouse, reserved amount, delivered/consumed/transferred amounts and, when tracked, per-identity reservation and delivery rows. Recompute location availability while excluding the current reservation. For a sales demand, compare it with remaining row demand after deliveries and other reservations. A packed component scales parent delivered stock units by component voucher quantity / parent stock quantity. For 5 bundles needing 3 pieces each, 2 bundles delivered and 4 pieces already reserved, only 15 − 6 − 4 = 5 pieces of row demand can be newly reserved even if the warehouse has plenty.

For source-to-target reservation handoff match item and warehouse, then allocate min(source open, target remainder) for the exact destination voucher row. For tracked items use per-identity undelivered quantity, carry named serials/batch quantities, and mark only the consumed source entitlement. The target receives a new reservation. Physical quantity and value do not move solely because of this handoff. On an actual tracked delivery allocate bounded quantities oldest first. Reversal returns them newest first, within each reservation's recorded limits. A reservation made via a pick list, already delivered in part/full, or carrying a positive delivered amount cannot be freely edited.

For a submitted record, positive transferred quantity has first priority: less than reserved → Partially Used, otherwise Closed. With no transfer, delivered + consumed ≥ reserved → Delivered. Next positive delivered → Partially Delivered; next positive consumed → Partially Used. Otherwise compare reserved to voucher quantity after precision rounding → Reserved or Partially Reserved. Cancelled or draft document state takes precedence over quantities. The status label alone cannot reconstruct shipment, production issue and handoff quantities. ## Downtime unit and reporting boundary

A downtime entry is per workstation/operator and must end after its start. Lock the workstation during overlap validation. Two intervals touching at a boundary can coexist. The stored duration is minutes. The report selects overlapping intervals allowed by the caller's record visibility and clips each to the requested reporting interval. Its output unit is **hours**. The chart sums per-row downtime rounded to two decimals by workstation. A recorded stop from 07:45 to 09:15 is 90 stored minutes. In the report bounded by 08:00 and 09:00 it contributes 1.00 hour. Distinguish this record from scheduled job-card capacity; the inspected downtime validator alone does not reserve a machine slot or post an expense.
