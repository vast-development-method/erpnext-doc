# Pricing decisions and customer reward entries

## Qualified product rules

Combine the product, parent/variant or group/brand match with the buy/sell context, company, transaction date, currency and party conditions before selecting a winner. A coupon-based rule must match the rule on the supplied coupon. Search item code, item group, then brand. When eligible rules specify priority the search can continue across these scopes. Without an explicit priority or multiple-applicable policy it can stop as soon as a scope yields a candidate. In the document-context branch an invalid condition removes that rule. A call lacking a document returns candidates without evaluating document conditions.

A normal line compares its stock quantity with the rule's quantity bounds multiplied by the rule-unit stock conversion. Compare the line's transaction quantity × price-list rate with the value bounds. Multiply the value bounds by the conversion factor **unless the transaction unit equals the rule unit**, in which case only the quantity comparison still uses conversion. Both nonzero maximums and minimums are inclusive; an absent/zero maximum is unbounded. Thus 2 boxes of 6 at 40/box satisfy a min of 2 boxes and amount 80 on a box-based offer. The measured quantity is 12, measured amount 80. For mixed/cumulative rules the decision replaces or augments these measures using the qualified document rows/history.

For the single-rule path prefer matching currency if a match exists among alternatives, then highest nonzero priority, then a matching price list for competing percentage discounts. An unresolved non-cart tie raises a conflict. The shopping-cart branch returns the first remaining candidate. For a multiple-rule set, eligible multiple-applicable rules are grouped in ascending integer priority with a default of one. Persist the applied rule identities so recalculation can distinguish paid inputs and generated rewards. For recursive free products sum the quantities of non-free rows matching the product and rule context. Subtract the recursion starting threshold. If the result is positive, multiply by free units per block divided by block size. With whole-block rounding enabled, take the floor of **the number of blocks** before multiplying by free units. When a nonzero starting threshold equals the rule minimum, add the base free quantity once. A nonpositive excess leaves the initial free quantity in place for a rule already qualified; the threshold filter occurs upstream. For 26 paid, start/minimum 10, block 5, reward 2 and rounding enabled the reward is (floor(16/5) × 2) + 2 = **8 units**. A generated reward line carries free-item identity, originating rule and configured reward rate (default zero). It must not feed back into that same quantity sum. ## Earn, redeem and unwind

Program tiers have spend thresholds and collection divisors. The lowest minimum must be zero. To earn on an eligible invoice, take signed grand total less the integer-converted monetary redemption on that invoice. Use that current amount plus historical spend (including expired entries in this tier calculation) to select the highest threshold reached. Subtract the absolute sum of submitted linked returns to obtain eligible amount. Points = integer truncation of eligible amount / tier divisor, with divisor 1 for a missing or zero factor. The program date must include the invoice date. Persist company, customer, program, tier, source invoice type/identity, eligible amount, integer points, posting date and expiry. For example a 206.75 invoice with 5.75 redeemed monetarily, 11.75 returned and divisor 10 gives current amount 201.75, eligible amount 190.00 and 19 points. The integer conversion on redeemed money affects the earnings calculation even when ledger settlement retains cents. Validate redemption against the company, nonnegative requested points, available balance as of the effective date, and currency value = points × conversion factor. This value cannot exceed the rounded total when enabled, otherwise grand total. The balance query includes only entries posted by that date and expiring on or after it. The sales invoice uses its posting date here. Debit the redemption expense account and credit the customer receivable by the applied monetary amount as part of invoice posting. Six points at 2.50 settle 15.00 of receivable. Preserve linked negative reward entries for the points. Consolidation must avoid a duplicate grant/redemption. A submitted linked return recalculates eligible earnings on its original invoice under the observed gates. Cancellation of earned points referenced by a later redemption is rejected in the reference deletion check. **Compatibility finding.** The lot selector orders positive, unexpired earning entries by expiry. Its separate redemption query sums negative entries per earning lot. The allocator subtracts that signed sum. A lot earned at 10 with an earlier −4 redemption is therefore locally treated as 14 available. The global signed balance still limits the request, but a second earning lot can allow the first lot to receive excessive attributed redemptions. Example: two eligible lots of 10 each, earlier −4 against lot 1, new request 8. Global 16 permits it. The observed lot arithmetic can allocate all 8 to lot 1. A corrected earliest-expiry policy would consume 6 from lot 1 and 2 from lot 2. Record the selected compatibility policy when integrating legacy balances. The lot selection also lacks the posting-date upper bound present on the global balance. These are source-derived observations, awaiting full-application fixtures with historical postings.
