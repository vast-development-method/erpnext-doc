# Green Checklist

[Business workflow and acceptance cases](../../../capabilities/guides/operations.md#facility-inspection-measurements).

Indictrans Tech lists Green Checklist for major version 13. Facilities teams use recurring checklists to record who performed a routine, who supervised it and when it happened. A task can apply to an asset or to a service in a common area. Common-area work can record a location. The publisher distinguishes a yes/no parameter from a numerical parameter with a specified range and advertises email, reports and dashboards. A release-level source revision remains unverified. ## Evidence for inspections and incident follow-up

The publisher’s checklist listing describes recurring activities, the performer, supervisor, location for common areas, binary answers and range readings. It also identifies email notifications, reporting and dashboards. A separate facilities case study follows an incident through an initial report, risk assessment and corrective follow-up. The case study names a particular client’s 24-hour, 48-hour and 30-day reporting stages. It supplies no automatic mapping from a missed checklist, a negative answer or an out-of-range measurement into that incident process. [Publisher’s checklist description](https://cloud.frappe.io/marketplace/apps/green_checklist) · [Publisher’s incident case study](https://www.indictranstech.com/case-studies/frappe-incident-management-system-a-smarter-approach-to-facilities-reporting/)

Evidence for the installed checklist would require a frequency definition and its resulting occurrences, a parameter definition with unit and boundary inclusivity, and a negative or out-of-range observation with the associated notification or decision. A screenshot of a status count alone cannot establish how the denominator treats missed, excused or reopened work. The [inspection contract](../../../capabilities/guides/operations.md#facility-inspection-measurements) gives an explicit recurrence, exception and reporting example for an independent ERP.
