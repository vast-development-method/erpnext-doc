# Employee recovery, lending accrual and allocation

## Manage an employee loan through payroll

The lending service owns principal, schedules, accruals, due demands and their settlement. The salary service owns the pay period, cash wage and the link from each slip loan line to its submitted repayment. An employee identifier, company and named loan must travel across that boundary. Read the original [payroll calculations](../../domains/payroll-and-benefits.md) for salary composition and how the payroll payable is credited.

| Operation | Required context | Result or rejection |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare obligations | Installed lending capability, employee, company, salary end date | Select submitted loans marked for salary recovery with a state other than Closed. For term loans prepare interest and, where available, due demands through that date. Without lending the guarded call returns immediately. |
| Price salary recovery | Current loan obligations at salary end | Populate an empty slip collection from loans with nonzero payable amounts. For each retained row reject a total payment above that loan's freshly calculated payable. Aggregate principal, interest and total payment independently. An edited total alone does not redistribute the two component fields. |
| Submit recovery | Nonzero row, slip posting date, salary end date, loan/product, employee, company, payroll payable | Create, save and submit a normal loan repayment. Book using the slip posting date and value it at salary period end. Use the batch payroll payable where there is a batch, otherwise use the company default. Store the repayment identity on the originating row. |
| Cancel recovery | Slip row with linked repayment | Cancel that precise repayment. Rows without a repayment identity have no cancellation action in this routine. |

A January slip posted on 2 February values the loan at 31 January. Given 200 of principal and 15 of interest due, a slip loan row of 216 fails review even if February interest has accrued. A cash payroll transfer for the employee must take the recovered amount into account exactly once. Replaying an interrupted submission needs a durable row-to-repayment association and a duplicate-effect test. The observed row handler itself does not establish replay safety. ## Accrue a term loan

The quote service consumes company day-count policy, pending principal, annual percentage and inclusive start and end dates. For the term helper, actual days equal the calendar difference plus one. Divide annual interest by 365 for Actual/365 and 30/365, by 360 for Actual/360 and 30/360, otherwise by the number of days in the year containing the posting date. For the two 30-day conventions the amount helper replaces the supplied count with 30, including when the dates cover a shorter interval. This is the observed helper's rule. A split across years must follow the selected upstream caller's accrual intervals.

For a 36,000 principal at 10% over 1–10 March on Actual/360, daily interest is 10 and the accrued amount is 100. On 30/360 the same helper produces 300. With Actual/Actual, 36,600 at 10% for one day in a leap posting year produces 10. The accrual entry builder defaults monetary precision to 2 when unset, rounds the amount for the positivity test, and creates only a positive rounded accrual. Preserve the owner, loan, base, rate, dates, accrual type, schedule and optional disbursement links.

For normal interest on a performing loan with per-document accounting enabled, debit the product's accrued-interest receivable and credit its interest income. Cancellation reverses the accounting path. Missing required accounts reject posting. The accounting-disabled branch omits the per-document posting. With monthly consolidation enabled the consolidate/reverse-delta process owns both submitted and cancelled amounts. The write-off date, non-performing asset suspense and penal/additional-interest accounts introduce separate decisions. Do not add the 100 to installment principal merely because interest accrued. ## Apply a payment to demands

A loan repayment records the loan, product, value date, repayment kind and amount tendered. Each allocation needs the specific outstanding demand, its component, allocated amount and any partner share. Written-off loans select the written-off collection order first. Specified settlement/adjustment types and qualifying settled loans select a settlement order next. Non-performing loans use the substandard order. Remaining loans use the standard order. The product can supply the offset order, with company configuration as fallback. Missing the chosen order on both product and company rejects the repayment.

Apply components in their configured sequence while funds remain. For example, with a term loan, two EMI demands (interest 20 and principal 100), a standard sequence whose first two categories are Normal then Principal, and a 70 normal repayment, consume interest 20 then principal 50. Remaining principal on the due demand is 50. Reordering the same categories so Principal comes first consumes 70 of principal and leaves the 20 interest demand open. Select the actual categories from configuration. The label Normal includes the interest subtype of EMI; the similarly named Interest category has a distinct partial-settlement path. Return unapplied cash to the enclosing repayment policy for its excess handling. A successful repayment must retain demand allocations so cancellation can undo the actual split at the original precision. ## Qualification boundary

The versioned catalogs include Loan Product, disbursement, collateral, repayment schedules, demand offset orders, write-off, classification and restructuring declarations. Full loss provisioning, partner settlement, consolidation deltas across a closed month, foreclosure, waiver allocation and concurrent backdated reposts need integrated tests before claiming interchangeable lending behaviour.

## Underwrite a secured application

Resolve the product within the application's company. An employee applicant must meet the employee-side company validation. A secured application values each proposed pledge using its quantity and supplied security price, or a valid price fetched for the security. Missing quantity or price rejects. Compute gross security value as quantity × price. For each row convert gross × (1 − haircut/100) to an integer before aggregating loanable value. With three units at 123.45 and a 15% haircut, gross is 370.35 and that row contributes 314 to the proposed ceiling. Applying the integer conversion only after aggregating multiple rows can change the allowed amount.

Check requested principal against every applicable nonzero ceiling: the product max, the proposed securities max and applicant exposure. The exposure test compares existing principal plus request with sanction × (1 + product tolerance/100). With a 100,000 sanction, 2% tolerance and 80,000 existing, a request of 22,000 meets the sanction threshold while 22,001 exceeds it. Product and collateral caps still apply independently. If a noncancelled loan already references the application, a later status change on the submitted application fails. The separate security assignment validates that at least one security is assigned, rejects duplicate security identities and sums gross and post-haircut values separately. For annual percentage r and positive monthly rate i=r/1200, require payment A greater than initial monthly interest P×i. Estimated periods = ceiling(log(A/(A−P×i)) / log(1+i)). At zero rate the branch stores P/A before the field's coercion. The calculation of an application estimate and the dated repayment schedule belong to different services; holidays, effective disbursement, broken-period interest and repricing require their own cash-flow cases.
